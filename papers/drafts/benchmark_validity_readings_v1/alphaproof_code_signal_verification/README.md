# The Abstract Carries Fewer Conditions Than the Body: Reading AlphaProof Code

*AI-assisted. Living research: staged, not peer reviewed, may be revised or withdrawn.*

**Scope of naming.** One document, "AlphaProof Code: Verifying Signal's Security at the Machine-Code Level" (dated 2026-11-10, 57 pp.; the paper names Google DeepMind and Google; the Lean development is released at the address the paper gives). The authors are called "the authors"; people named in its acknowledgments and in the works it cites are not named here. No intent is attributed to anyone.

Read from an operator-supplied copy (57 pp.; PDF sha256 beginning cf813b7192ed247d): Sections 1 to 9 in full, Table 1 and Figures 1 to 5; the appendices (about 25 pages of model definitions, transition tables and assumption lists) by headings and searches for axioms, hooks and cost, not line by line. ## What it is
A framework in which a human and agents write a Lean specification and Lean semantics of AArch64 machine code, a team of prover agents produces proofs, and a gatekeeper accepts a proof only if the Lean kernel checks it and the `comparator` tool confirms it proves exactly a held-out copy of the specification. Applied to libsignal v0.105.4 (one-to-one sessions; the post-quantum PQXDH handshake and the Triple Ratchet) built with the stock Rust compiler for AArch64 inside a command-line harness the authors wrote. Headline: about 6.5 million lines of proof on about 26,000 trusted lines (18,000 of statements and definitions, 8,000 of semantics); ten symbolic theorems and a computational bound for classical attackers; two historical bugs re-introduced and shown to violate the symbolic theorem; and "the first proofs of protocol-level security properties for the unmodified code of a widely deployed implementation of a cryptographic protocol".

## Flags (ranked; post hoc)
1. **What is verified is narrower than the abstract's wording, and the body says so.** The object is the authors' own command-line harness, one operation per run, state in files, randomness from a `coins` file of fixed size, fixed clock. Thirty-nine routines are replaced by contracts at the binary boundary (SHA-256 and SHA-512 compression, AES-256-CBC, the X25519 and Edwards operations, Kyber1024, ML-KEM-768, HMAC, five libc memory and string routines, the two output functions, a few Rust library routines); the cryptographic primitives are idealised, and the other contracts "are not proved against the real routines". Key generation is unverified; the two parties are honest with fixed roles; an authentic key infrastructure is assumed (trust-on-first-use is not exercised); one session per file; the responder has at most one one-time prekey and one-time Kyber prekeys are not covered; inputs and the number of sends are bounded; an exact replay's loss of forward secrecy reported in prior work is not ruled out by the theorem. "Side channels and other secrets in memory are out of scope". Table 1 and Section 5.1.6 state all this; the abstract does not carry it. (C5; E1)
2. **The post-quantum content is partial.** Handshake secrecy against a future attacker who breaks Diffie-Hellman is symbolic, with idealised primitives; the computational theorem is for classical attackers; the ratchet theorems let the attacker choose the post-quantum ratchet's keys, so they show the Triple Ratchet is no weaker than the Double Ratchet, "not the post-quantum healing that SPQR adds". The abstract names the post-quantum handshake and the Triple Ratchet among the proved subjects. (E1)
3. **The computational bound goes vacuous at modest scale, and two idealisations stay in the main theorem.** If nearly every message starts a new epoch the bound is vacuous for one session of about 25,000 messages or for 2^20 sessions of 2^10 messages (about 1.07 billion messages in total). The handshake key-derivation function is treated as a random oracle in one clause ("This is a heuristic"), and two non-standard joint-security assumptions are kept because deriving them from the standard one "would weaken the bound considerably". All stated in 5.2.4 and 5.2.5. (C4; E1)
4. **"Cheap" is shown in labour, not in resources.** Five people for four weeks (about 20 person-weeks, about 4.6 person-months); 40 to 80 agents per campaign for 24 to 72 hours on one machine; clean builds of several hours. No compute, token or dollar totals appear in the text read, and "years of expert labor with earlier tools" is asserted without a baseline; the comparison point named in the introduction (a microkernel verified in twenty person-years) is a different artifact. The paper's own lesson is that "the majority of our effort went not into proofs but into specifications", where effort is not quantified. (C3; E1/E2)
5. **Proof volume is a size metric, not a strength metric.** Of the 6.5 million proof lines, 5.3 million are about the binary, and by the authors' account "a substantial fraction of our proofs concerns registers, stack frames and memory layout". The lines say how much machine code was reasoned about, not how strong the ten symbolic theorems or the bound are. (C1; E1/E2)
6. **A post-hoc check is allowed to move its reference toward the artifact it checks, and the counts are not in the text read.** The amendment rule lets a proof campaign amend the trusted specification when proofs fail, under conditions (necessity shown by a machine-checked disproof, smallness, logging, a critic that never sees proof attempts, human approval) and says "A neutral amendment must bring the specification closer to the code" while a security-weakening change is "raised to a human as a potential bug". The guards are sound practice. The number of accepted amendments, their direction and the number escalated as potential bugs are not stated in the text read (they may be in the released log; not opened). The authors also report that agent-written specifications "were hard to audit" and needed human rewrites for legibility, so part of the trusted base is a readability problem. (E1; E3 for the log)
7. **The generalisation to deployed applications is conditional.** The apps run the same Rust code on AArch64; the theorems carry over "as long as we trust that" other environments preserve the harness's behaviour. The proofs concern the authors' build run as a fresh process. (E1)

## What holds
Specification discipline is the strongest part: vacuity and satisfiability proofs, mutation testing against the binary (for example skipping the authentication-tag check), two re-introduced historical bugs with complete Lean disproofs, a held-out specification copy checked by the gatekeeper, `sorry` and `native_decide` rejected, only standard axioms, catalogues of departures from the literature (over 200 computational and about 35 symbolic entries), 15 gaps reported in a published computational analysis and errors in printed queries of a prior symbolic analysis, with repairs; the semantics is tested in lockstep against two independent oracles and an emulator; the Rust compiler is not in the trusted base; Table 1 puts attacker, guarantee, assumptions, trusted model and what is not covered side by side for both models; the limitations are listed in Sections 5.1.6, 5.2.5 and 9; the Lean development is open-sourced for community review; related work is fair about narrower prior verifications.

## Scorecard against the predictions
9 of 12 met (2 in part), 3 not met. The three misses are where the paper is more careful than predicted: specification validation, testing of the semantics, and the compiler's position outside the trusted base. Detail in `predictions_fixed_2026-11-10.md`.

**Deeper pass:** recommended and cheap: take the released amendment log and classify each accepted amendment as stronger, neutral toward the code or weakening, and count the escalations; check whether the 39 contracts' non-cryptographic part has independent validation beyond the Figure 5 scenarios; ask for compute and token totals. Not done.

## Limitations: what this does and does not establish
- One reader; the flags are leads tied to a page, table or section, not verdicts, and the paper or its artifact may answer them in places not read.
- It establishes what the text says its theorems cover and what it states about cost. It does not establish that the specifications match Signal's intent (a judgment for protocol experts) or that any theorem is wrong.
- The number of flags is not a severity ranking (see the series overview). Most flags here are about how the headline compresses a carefully conditioned body, not about the proofs.
