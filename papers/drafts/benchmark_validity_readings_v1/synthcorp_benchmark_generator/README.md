# Gold Checked Against the Generator's Own Log: Reading SynthCorp, a Benchmark Generator for Data Agents

*AI-assisted. Living research: staged, not peer reviewed, may be revised or withdrawn.*

**Scope of naming.** One paper, "SynthCorp: A Platform for Creating Benchmarks for Data Agents" (arXiv:2611.14765v1, 10 Nov 2026, 32 pp.; the author block names Google). The authors are called "the authors"; works the paper cites are described by role. The paper evaluates three models (Gemma 4 26B-A4B, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Claude Opus 5.5) and uses Claude Opus 5.5 also as a judge of its pipeline, so a model from the assisting model's developer appears twice in the paper. No intent is attributed to anyone.

**Disclosure.** AI-assisted (Claude, working under this project's own non-default standard; direction, selection and release decisions are the operator's); single reader. Because the paper names a model from the same developer as the assisting model, in two roles (judge and evaluated agent), the reading says so here and in flag 3. Read from an operator-supplied copy (32 pp.; PDF sha256 beginning 20a64733eb48bc6f; not stored here): every page as images, tables parsed from the text layer, and a text search for method and release statements. No code or data was available to read or run.

## What it is
A pipeline that turns a business scenario and requested "complexity dimensions" (business glossary, implicit business rules, multi-granularity tables, table redundancy, schema drift) into a simulated data estate (relational tables, documents, a data catalog) and a hidden simulation log of every operation. Tasks and executable "gold programs" are then derived from the log by an LLM panel that debates each candidate against the log, and audited against it. The first release is seven estates (10 to about 1,000 operational tables) and 256 tasks in five domains, three scenarios used to develop the implementation and four held out. Claims in the abstract: the estates pass deterministic pipeline checks with "a near-zero error rate"; every requested dimension is covered; the benchmarks separate three models and four data-context settings; and gold programs are verified "without manual annotation".

## Flags (ranked; post hoc; leads, not verdicts)
1. **The gold is verified against the generator's own record.** Gold programs are derived by a debate among LLM agents "against" the simulation log (Section 4.4, p. 12) and audited against the same log, "independently of the LLMs that derived it" (Section 3.5.2, p. 9). The log is the pipeline's record of what its own LLM-executed primitives did, so "verified" means consistent with the generator, not with a reference outside it; the paper says of the data itself that "The actual data values in the databases are much less important." (Section 6, p. 19). The independence holds for the deriving LLMs, not for the log. (E2; E3 for the reading)
2. **The outside check offered for the gold is agreement of other LLMs, after a rescoring.** The paper reports that state-of-the-art agents "match them on up to 94% of runs after accounting for format differences" (abstract). In the multi-step file setting (S4), "Counting these predictions as correct raises S4 accuracy to 94% for Opus and 90% for DeepSeek." (p. 18); the unrescored values are 88.8 and 77.7 (Table 7, p. 17), a gain of about 13 and 31 task-equivalents of 256. How the format-failure shares (56% and 61% of failed runs) were determined is not stated; the one manual audit covers three tasks (53 of 54 failed runs correct apart from format). Agreement between LLMs from overlapping training distributions is weak verification of a reference derived by LLMs. (E1 for the arithmetic; E2)
3. **One model is both judge and top-ranked agent; the panel is unnamed.** Claude Opus 5.5 is the LLM judge for the five semantic pipeline-error types (Appendix B, p. 28) and the best-scoring evaluated agent in every setting on the pooled score (Table 7). The judge scores the pipeline, not the agents' answers, which limits self-preference to the pipeline-error table; no human agreement or error rate is reported for the judge. The "multiple distinct LLMs" in the gold-derivation panel are not named, so whether an evaluated model also derived or judged its own gold is not stated. Six of the seven estates are generated with one Gemini model and the seventh with another (Section 5.1, p. 13). (E2)
4. **The saturation motivation and the first release pull against each other.** The case for the platform is that existing benchmarks "saturate quickly" (abstract); in this release the top model scores 86.6 (agent with search tools) and 88.8 (agent with files) pooled, 94.6 and 94.0 on two estates, and 94% on the rescored runs, and the conclusion says "many of the generated tasks are relatively simple for frontier models" (p. 21). The discriminating part is the 101 composite tasks, and the evidence shown is one task on which all 12 runs of one model fail (p. 19) and 11 multi-rule composite tasks on one estate where the agent-with-search-tools setting scores 91% (Opus), 76% (DeepSeek) and 33% (Gemma). (E1/E2)
5. **The "held-out" estates share the development estates' domain and complexity library.** The development scenarios are Retail-10, Banking and Logistics; the held-out ones are Retail-100, Retail-1000, Music store and Insurance (Table 4, p. 13). Retail-100 and Retail-1000 scale up the same coffee-shop scenario and share its "counter-sales core" (pp. 13, 20), and the five requested complexity dimensions come from one library built before the held-out runs. Generalisation to a new domain or a new kind of complexity is not tested; Retail-1000 also uses a different generator model from the other six. (E2)
6. **"Near-zero error" is scoped to the nine deterministic checks and counted per invocation.** The Retail-1000 rate of 0.2% (33 of 15.7K, Table 5, p. 14) is one daily write that fails on every one of 28 days (28 of the 33, note b) plus a few empty-upstream cascades; counted once, about 0.04%. The semantic checks, not quoted in the abstract, show 6.2% (six estates) and 12.7% (all seven) of specified dependencies violated and 3.6% and 7.1% operator-contract violations (p. 15). These do not alter gold, which is computed on the materialised data, but they bear on how consistent the estates are. (E1)
7. **No code, data or release statement, and the verification labels are not counted.** A text search of the PDF finds none; the only repositories named are a third party's sample database. A task is labelled "log-verified", "corroborated" or "unverified" (pp. 10, 32) but the counts per label for the 256 tasks are not reported, so how much of the benchmark rests on direct log confirmation is not visible. (E2)
8. **The separation claim is an ordering of point estimates.** Opus > DeepSeek > Gemma holds on the pooled score in each of the four settings; the two agentic settings are not separated for any model (Opus 86.6 against 88.8 with standard errors of 1.9 and 1.7), DeepSeek is higher than Opus on one estate in one setting (Banking, files setting: 91.0 against 87.2) and the order is not monotone on Music store with schema only; no paired test is reported. (E1)

## What holds
Every number checked recomputes: task counts (256; 155 atomic and 101 composite; 205 requiring a document or catalog), the share of tasks whose requested dimension comes from a requested disruption (119 of 198, 60.1%), the distinct-skeleton share (235 of 256), the error rates on the six smaller estates (6.2%, 3.6%), and each pooled score in Table 7 as the task-weighted mean of its seven estates within 0.1; the S1-to-S2 and S2-to-S3 gains match the stated ranges. The paper separates the pipeline's correctness from the realism of its values, reports its own semantic errors, states the boundary of the simulation (Section 6) and compares simulated and real data on a set of checks, including where they differ (Table 8, p. 20). The design is a real engineering advance over static snapshots in what it makes available: every complexity traces to a logged event, which is what lets the authors build tasks no one annotated.

## Limitations: what this does and does not establish
- One reader; the flags are leads tied to a page or table, not verdicts, and the paper may answer them in places not read.
- It establishes what the printed tables and text support and that they are arithmetically consistent. It does not establish that any gold program is wrong (none was re-derived), that the data estates are unrealistic beyond what the paper states, or anything about the code (not available).
- The standard errors are from three runs per task over 256 tasks; the comparisons in flag 8 are arithmetic on printed values, not tests with a stated null.
- The evaluated models were not independently checked.
