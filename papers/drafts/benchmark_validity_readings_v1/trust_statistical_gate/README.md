# A Gate With a Stated Error Rate, Graded on Its Own Terms: Reading TRUST

*AI-assisted. Living research: staged, not peer reviewed, may be revised or withdrawn.*

**Scope of naming.** One paper, "Trustworthy Recursive Self-Improvement with Statistical Guarantees" (arXiv:2611.10787v1, stat.ML, 7 November 2026, 44 pp.; the author block names Zhejiang University and George Washington University). The authors are called "the authors". The paper's experiments use two models (DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Qwen-3.8-Flash) and three public benchmarks (HotpotQA, DROP, APPS). No model from the assisting model's developer appears in the paper.

**Disclosure.** AI-assisted (Claude, working under this project's own non-default standard; direction, selection and release decisions are the operator's); single reader. Read from an operator-supplied copy (PDF sha256 af27e023025d8c25c79948a9ebfa73bd6c122a0d6a01303cddfb491dd6811869; the PDF is not stored here). All 44 pages were read, at different depths (see the tier line).

## What it is
A statistical acceptance gate, TRUST, for loops in which an agent edits its own harness (reasoning rules, retrieval routing, memory, workflow; 20 operators; the solver model fixed). The gate controls a "false evolution rate" (the expected share of accepted updates whose true effect is a loss beyond a tolerance) across rounds, using an online multiple-testing procedure with e-values, a noninferiority margin and a correction for reusing validation data, and an LLM that scales the evidence required for each candidate from the loop's history. The paper states a theorem that the rate stays below a budget under an assumption on the data-reuse bias (Assumption 1), and, asymptotically, that improving candidates are accepted and a lower bound on final gain. Experiments: 30 rounds, 5 seeds, a 200-example validation pool (up to 40 drawn per round, overlapping across rounds), 300 held-out test examples used only for reporting; four baselines (a greedy gate, a single-round e-process gate, and two family-wise-error gates). Headline in the abstract: the greedy gate's average ratio of accepted inferior updates is 45.59% and TRUST's is 2.78%, "while achieving higher final scores than the state-of-the-art statistical gate baselines".

## Flags (ranked; post hoc)
1. **The safety result holds in the paper's own metric, and the price is paid in acceptance.** The greedy gate exceeds 30% zero-tolerance false-evolution rate in six of six settings and TRUST stays at or below it in six of six (the abstract's 45.59% to 2.78% recomputes from Table 1, p. 8). TRUST's share of improving candidates accepted is 0.19 to 0.45 times the greedy gate's in every setting (mean 14.3% against 50.6%); the abstract does not say so. A zero rate is also reached by accepting nothing, which is what one baseline does (it "never accepts an update", p. 8) and another nearly does (three updates across all runs). (E1)
2. **The gain lead is inside the noise against the one working baseline.** TRUST's mean final gain is 24.84 points; the single-round e-process gate's is 22.40 and the greedy gate's is 23.64. TRUST is higher than the e-process gate in six of six settings, but the difference over its combined standard error (5 seeds) is under 2 in five of six; against the greedy gate it is higher in four of six, within noise in three, and lower in two (by 4.77 points on DROP with one model, z -1.8; by 1.50 points on APPS with the other, z -4.4). The comparison set for "highest" is chosen by the authors' own criterion before ranking (methods above 30% are shaded and excluded from the bolding). The two family-wise-error gates accept almost nothing at the stated sample sizes, so only one baseline is a working comparator; the paper says these "are conservative in practice" (p. 8). (E1/E2)
3. **The starting point is a deliberately broken harness, and the abstract does not say so.** The body states that every gate starts from a harness that "contains prespecified weaknesses that provide clear opportunities for improvement" (Appendix G.1, p. 30). In the HotpotQA example the starting reasoning rule says "Treat every item as asking for the first title shown in the context" and "Return the first displayed title as the answer" (Appendix H.2, p. 39). Gains of 7.87 to 36.24 points start from that. A condition disclosed in the body and not carried into the abstract; the claim reads as an existence statement conditional on a planted-weakness start, two small models and three public benchmarks. (E2)
4. **A candidate can raise its own validation score by storing validation labels, and the paper's allowance for it is untested.** The overfitting example (Appendix I, pp. 41-42) shows the proposer adding a memory entry that holds the full validation question, its verified answer and the supporting facts, with the procedure "Reuse a verified answer only when the current question is identical." On the repeated question the candidate returns the stored answer. The authors present this as the reason for the data-reuse correction; the held-out test set is unaffected. The correction strength is set to 0.01 by hand; the paper says it "does not address how to identify an exact value of overlap correction strength from data", and the correction assumption is not tested. By arithmetic on the paper's own settings (a pool of 200, one to three stored cases per candidate) 0.01 is the same order as the one-step leakage the memory operator can produce (about 0.003 to 0.008 per draw, 0.028 for ten stored cases); the paper does not derive it. In the vocabulary of the published gate audit this is the provenance of the data the gate scores. (E1 arithmetic, E2 text, E3 whether other operators or settings leak differently)
5. **The proved criterion tolerates a large per-update loss.** The tolerance (defined on p. 3; default 0.10) is on a score scale of 0 to 1, a loss of 10 points per accepted update, against gains of 8 to 36 points over 30 rounds. The zero-tolerance rate in the abstract is an empirical diagnostic ("FER0 counts accepted non-improving updates, including ties"), not what the theorem controls (p. 31). With a tolerance of zero the gate accepts nothing (gain 0.00, Table 8, p. 38), and the default sits just below a cliff: the zero-tolerance rate is 10.0% at 0.10 and 57.68% at 0.15. (E1/E2)
6. **The defaults sit at the grid optimum of the reported metric.** On the one setting where the sensitivity grids were run, the default budget, tolerance and correction strength (0.30, 0.10, 0.01) are the gain-maximising value in each grid, subject, for the tolerance, to the paper's own 30% condition. The grids report the same test-based gain, zero-tolerance rate and acceptance rate as the main table, and no separate tuning set is described, although the held-out set is said to be "used only for reporting" (p. 30; grids on pp. 37-38). The defaults are then used unchanged in the other five settings, which is in the paper's favour. (E1 for the pattern, E3 for the procedure)
7. **The proved lower bounds on power and gain are asymptotic, and their explicit constant is vacuous at the experiments' sample size.** The bounds hold as the validation pool and the per-round sample grow with the number of rounds fixed; the abstract (p. 1) says "we further derive a lower bound on the final performance gain" without the qualifier, which appears with the theorem (Theorem 3 and its discussion, p. 7). The proof gives an explicit constant (Appendices D.1 and D.2, pp. 21-24); evaluated at the main configuration (tolerance 0.10, null boundary 0.45, the one in-range betting fraction 0.10 of the mixture) it is 6.35e-5, so the factor that multiplies the available improvement is 0.0025 at 40 draws per round; about 10,900 draws would be needed to halve the miss bound, and the proof requires at least about 968 per round whatever the unreported allocator constants are (the pool has 200). The data-reuse exposure bound equals 1 after round 6. These are worst-case numbers for arbitrarily small improvements; the empirical rates are not contradicted by a vacuous bound. The error-rate guarantee itself is the sound half. (E1 arithmetic on the paper's formulas; E3 for the proofs beyond the steps read)
8. **The allocator's benefit is not shown.** Final scores are 44.91 +- 1.36 with the LLM allocator and 43.78 +- 1.18 without, a difference of 1.13 against a combined standard error of 1.80 (z 0.63), in one setting; the paper writes that this "suggests a performance benefit" (p. 9). The mechanism is shown with one seed. The allocator reads the proposer's own text ("expected benefit", "possible regression"); the multiplier is clipped and the error-rate theorem is stated not to rely on the LLM being right, which bounds the channel. (E1/E2)
9. **Reproduction.** The appendix says the evaluation procedure "can be found in the code" (p. 39); the PDF gives no link or availability statement, and the allocator's constants (maximum level, minimum and maximum spending fraction) are not reported in the text read. There is no discussion of contamination for the three public benchmarks, and none of the labelling noise in "improving" and "non-improving" when judged on 300 test examples. (E2)

## What holds
The paper is explicit about its assumptions and says that guarantees hold "under the stated assumptions"; the limitations paragraph names the unidentified correction strength, a guarantee that can be conservative, and the allocator's mistakes. The error-rate proof is a standard online false-discovery-rate argument that is valid given Assumption 1 (the testing level is fixed from pre-round information and the spent budget sums to at most the budget). The held-out test set is used for reporting only, methods share the initial responses and cached outputs, seeds and standard errors are reported, the sensitivity of all three parameters is shown, and the greedy gate's failure, which is the point of the paper, is large and consistent. The improver cannot edit the gate, the scorer, the validation pool or the allocator (Tables 2 to 5); the proposer prompt says to "Preserve the fixed solver role, output contract, and single-call execution protocol." The paper says the data-reuse problem is outside its scope: "We do not develop a complete model of validation data reuse." (p. 14)

## Limitations: what this does and does not establish
- One reader; the flags are leads tied to a page or table, not verdicts, and a paper may answer them in places not read.
- The standard errors are over five seeds that share data splits across methods; the z values are arithmetic, not tests with a stated null.
- It establishes what the paper's tables, text and formulas support. It does not establish that the gate fails or works outside the stated setup, that the proofs are wrong, or anything about the code, which was not available.
- The number of flags is not a severity ranking (see the series overview).
