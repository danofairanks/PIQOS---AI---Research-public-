## What it is
A benchmark for protein-ligand docking and structure prediction without a given pocket: Astex Diverse (stated n = 85), PoseBusters Benchmark (130 of 308 after a training-cutoff filter), DockGen-E (stated n = 122, the authors' variant of DockGen with full assemblies) and CASP15 (19 complexes, 102 fragment ligands; 6 single-ligand and 13 multi-ligand complexes). Seven deep-learning methods and one conventional pipeline (P2Rank with AutoDock Vina) are run three times each; docking baselines receive AlphaFold 3-predicted apo structures. Headline: co-folding methods "generally outperform comparable conventional and DL docking baseline algorithms" while being challenged by novel poses; AlphaFold 3 depends on input alignments more than Chai-1 and Boltz-1; and methods "struggle to strike a balance" between structural accuracy and a new interaction-fingerprint metric.

## Flags (ranked; post hoc)
1. **The scored test sets are smaller than the stated ones, and the gap is not explained in the text read.** Recounting the printed rates from the released per-complex files: on Astex they reproduce exactly when divided by 84, not 85 (DiffDock-L 52.8 / 51.2 / 13.5, P2Rank-Vina 7.5 / 4.4 / 3.6, AlphaFold 3 69.0 / 64.3 / 54.8 for cRMSD <= 1 A, RMSD <= 2 A, and RMSD <= 2 A with PoseBusters validity; the released set has 85 complexes and one, 1YV3_BIT, is never scored). On DockGen-E they reproduce at 100, not 122 (AlphaFold 3 54.0 / 51.0 and Boltz-1 31.3 / 32.0; AlphaFold 3 scores exactly 100 of the 122 test ids, 22 never). The validity-gated row on DockGen-E gives 43.3 and 29.0 against 44.3 and 29.3 printed once the identity checks are left out; with every check applied it is near zero, so the paper's validity definition on that set is not the full list. PoseBusters (130) was not recounted. The text, Table 1 and the figure captions give 85 and 122. Rankings are unaffected (all methods share the denominator); the stated sample size is. (C1; E1 arithmetic on released data, E3 for any explanation in unread appendices)
2. **Missing predictions are counted as failures, so failure to run and failure to dock are mixed.** The paper says a prediction that errors in scoring "is excluded from the evaluation", but the printed rates divide by the common 84 or 100: scored rows per run are Astex AlphaFold 3 84, Boltz-1 61, DiffDock-L 75-77, P2Rank-Vina 50-53, and DockGen-E AlphaFold 3 100, Boltz-1 60-89, DiffDock-L 58-59, P2Rank-Vina 27-28. That is the fairer rule, and it means the "strong conventional (blind) docking baseline" scores 4.4% on Astex partly because about 40% of its runs produce no prediction (7.2% over its scored rows). The paper's wording on exclusion and its computation differ. (C4; E1)
3. **The headline rankings are within sampling uncertainty at these sizes.** Unpaired Wilson 95% intervals at the scored sizes: PoseBusters AlphaFold 3 38.1% (30.2-46.7) against Boltz-1 34.4% (26.8-42.9), z = 0.62; Astex AlphaFold 3 54.8% against Chai-1 44.4%, z = 1.36; DockGen-E AlphaFold 3 44.3% against Boltz-1 29.3%, z = 2.23. The text reads Boltz-1 as "approaching" AlphaFold 3 on PoseBusters; the difference cannot be resolved there. The plotted +/- is standard deviation over three runs, not over complexes, and paired outcomes are not reported. (C2, C3; E2)
4. **The "chemical specificity" metric is relative, and its construction is not shown to be meaningful.** PLIF-WM is rescaled with the best and worst values among the compared methods ("normalized relative to each collection of the latest baseline methods"), so the best method on each set scores 1 and the worst 0 whatever the absolute agreement, values change when methods are added, and they are not comparable across datasets. The underlying PLIF-EMD treats a histogram of interaction-fingerprint counts as a 1D discrete distribution over unified bins and takes a Wasserstein distance; the text read gives no ordering or ground distance for those categories, and an earth mover's distance over arbitrarily ordered categories is not defined without one (the equation's fraction did not extract, so its exact form is E3). The accuracy-versus-specificity finding rests on this metric. (E1/E2)
5. **The comparison of co-folding with docking is confounded by inputs and conditions.** Every docking baseline receives AlphaFold 3-predicted apo structures aligned to the holo pocket with PyMOL, and AlphaFold 3 is the leading co-folding method; on CASP15 only 36.84% of those inputs (20.00% of the public ones) have RMSD < 4 A and TM-score > 0.7, against 59.09% on PoseBusters. The conventional baseline is a blind, apo pipeline whose pocket step and Vina scoring operate on predicted structures; Boltz-1 is run with its inference-time physical potentials ("making its predictions slightly slower but considerably more physically plausible"); a newer Boltz model is excluded to keep the time split. All of this is stated in the text or appendices; the abstract's "comparable conventional and DL docking baseline algorithms" does not carry it. (E1/E2)
6. **Version drift changed what the headline is.** The first version found that template-based docking performs about as well as recent single-ligand deep-learning docking on multi-ligand targets; the fourth said deep-learning methods outperform conventional docking consistently while still including the template-based method; the seventh, retitled, says co-folding methods generally outperform and contains no mention of the template-based method (term counts 4, 13, 0). Apo inputs changed from ESMFold to AlphaFold 3, Astex 80 to 85 complexes, PoseBusters 280 (28 dropped because the folding model could not run) to 308 with a 130 subset. No note in v7's text explains the removal of the conventional method that was competitive in the first version. (C6; E1 for counts, the reason is not stated)
7. **The multi-ligand set is small and its counts disagree across sections.** 13 multi-ligand complexes (Wilson half-width about 24 points at 50%); 4 of 19 complexes are confidential; Appendix A gives 15 public (4 single, 11 multi) while Appendix D.4's parentheses read "6 (2) and 13 (2)"; the 102 fragment ligands include single-atom ions and the text read does not say whether the success rate pools them. (C5; E1)
8. **The novelty analysis uses a small selected subset with uncorrected tests.** n = 41 of the 130 PoseBusters complexes, Pearson and Spearman per method; the authors state the p-values are unadjusted; similarity is measured against the pre-cutoff PDB. Explanations such as one for AlphaFold 3's alignment dependence are hedged ("one potential explanation"). (E1)

## What holds
The time-split reasoning is explicit (cutoff dates per model; the newer Boltz model excluded for it); the Astex training overlap is stated plainly; three runs per method; the PoseBusters validity gate is shown for every method (DiffDock-L drops from 29.3% to 7.2% when validity is required); runtimes and memory are reported; code, data and predictions are released, per-complex result files included, and the printed rates reproduce to rounding from them; the conclusion is hedged ("generally", "challenged").

## Limitations: what this does and does not establish
- One reader; the flags are leads tied to a page, table or released file, not verdicts, and the paper may explain them in places not read.
- It establishes that the printed rates on two sets are computed over 84 and 100 complexes, not the stated 85 and 122, for the methods recounted; it does not establish that results would change with the full sets, only what the denominators are. It does not establish that any method's ranking is wrong.
- The number of flags is not a severity ranking (see the series overview).
