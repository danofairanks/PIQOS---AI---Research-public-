# A Contrast That Mixes Sequence With Format: Reading the Path-Dependent Triage Study

*AI-assisted. Living research: staged, not peer reviewed, may be revised or withdrawn.*

**Scope of naming.** One paper, "Path-dependent triage: Early judgments lead conversational AI chatbots to miss mental-health emergencies" (arXiv:2611.09647v1; 63 pp. with supplement; the paper names the Max Planck UCL Centre for Computational Psychiatry and Ageing Research, the UK AI Security Institute, the University of Oxford and Microsoft AI). The authors are called "the authors". No individual is named and no intent is attributed to anyone.

**Disclosure.** AI-assisted (Claude, working under this project's own non-default standard; direction, selection and release decisions are the operator's); single reader. Models from the assisting model's developer appear in the paper's pipeline: one plays the simulated user, writes the case checklists and rewrites the matched message, another does all the scoring, and three are among the 13 chatbots tested; the reading says where each bears on a flag, with that framework context. Read from an operator-supplied copy (PDF sha256 beginning 092b512674f874fb) via text extraction: all pages, including the supplement's prompts and tables; figure plots by caption only. The data and code the paper promises "with publication of the peer-reviewed manuscript" were not available. Reading lens: the series overview, plus the five questions added on 2026-11-09. Predictions were fixed before the body was read (`predictions_fixed_2026-11-09.md`).

## What it is
13 chatbot endpoints held 10-turn conversations with an LLM-simulated user seeded by 112 mental-health case files (28 per urgency level A-D, generated by a language model, reviewed by one author-psychiatrist); 1,451 completed conversations. Emergency (D-level) under-triage is 20.3% in conversation, 5.6% when the whole case is one message, and 12.1% when the facts the conversation surfaced are rewritten as one message (n = 354 paired D-level cases). "Inference anchoring" (a lagged ordinal model) and "context-seeking collapse" (clustered question scripts; 41.7% against 26.7% repetition for clinicians) are the proposed mechanisms.

## Flags (ranked; post hoc)
1. **The 12.1% contrast does not separate sequence from format.** The matched message is built from the simulated user's turns only and rewritten by a model into one message: the chatbot's questions are removed, and so is the filler (59.8% of user messages carried no new decisive fact). No order-shuffle control and no injected-early-judgment control appear. By the paper's own ablation, deleting the chatbot's questions moves emergency under-triage from 22.7% to 18.9% (3.8 of the 10.6 points to the matched message, about a third, across separate analyses; C1); the rest sits in how the user's information is laid out. A reader that integrates dispersed, redundant transcripts poorly would show this without anchoring on its own earlier judgments. (E1; reading E2)
2. **"Inference anchoring" is confounded by input overlap.** The anchor (the previous turn's disposition) is a prediction from a transcript that is the current one minus a single exchange, and most exchanges add nothing; agreement with the previous turn must rise, and new facts must move a posterior less, for a reader insensitive to order. The glossary's own measure is the fraction of turns that repeat the previous disposition. No order-invariant benchmark is given for how much the weight on the previous turn should rise (+1.92 at turn 2 to +3.52 at turn 10). Separating dispositions into independent calls that never see earlier dispositions is a strength, and it moves the question from self-consistency to input redundancy. (E1; reading E2)
3. **One headline pools 13 endpoints from 3.6% to 82.1%.** Dropping the worst endpoint alone moves an equal-weight mean from 20.3% to about 15% (C2, approximate); "frontier" covers older endpoints; the three best sit at 3.6%. (E1/E2)
4. **The headline test treats pairs as independent.** An exact McNemar test on 354 case-by-chatbot pairs that repeat 28 cases across 13 chatbots (C3); the paper's other analyses use mixed models. (E1/E2)
5. **Ground truth and "clinician-validated".** Labels are generated by a language model; one author-psychiatrist reviewed every case and excluded none; the independent panel (50 registered doctors, one psychiatrist and seven general practitioners among them, about 1.7 minutes per case, no adjudication) agrees at Fleiss kappa 0.502 and 62 of 112 cases are clear (C5), 29 of them D against 28 prespecified. The abstract says each case has a clinician-validated level. (E1)
6. **Scorer validity.** Information disclosure, question codes and disclosure categories are scored by one model; the reported agreement (kappa 0.916 to 0.98) is across three replicates of that scorer, which the paper's own reporting checklist calls "inter-replicate reliability", and no human-versus-scorer figure appears in the text or the supplement. The checklist was built so that the label is inferable from the facts; saturation then does not predict accuracy at all (OR 1.02, CI 0.92 to 1.13), with a narrow range (median 87.5%). (E1/E2)
7. **Family overlap in the instrument.** The simulator, checklist writer, rewriter and scorer come from one developer family, three targets do too, and the case generator is a second developer's model that is also a target. No cross-family simulator or scorer check is reported. The direction of any bias is unknown. (E1)
8. **The simulator's disclosure rule drives elicitation.** The user simulator is told to disclose conservatively and to reveal specific details only in answer to a specific direct question; context-seeking "collapse" is measured against a user who withholds by design, and realism is supported by a citation, not a test here. (E1/E2)
9. **A single-sided headline metric with a cheap extreme solution.** The three endpoints at 3.6% emergency under-triage include GPT-5.4 (turn-10 mean error +0.54, macro accuracy 45.5%, -2.7 points over turns) and GPT-5 (+0.46, 50.0%); Claude Sonnet 4.6 also sits at 3.6% with +0.38 and 61.6%, the best combination (C6). The paper's own regression finds safer-on-emergency chatbots over-triage more. A benchmark scored on emergency under-triage alone can be improved by escalating everything; over-triage costs are named, not measured. (E1/E2)
10. **A turn-1 accuracy of 4.5% for one endpoint** (Table S5; chance with four levels is 25%) suggests a parsing or format effect; failure causes for the 5 incomplete conversations are not reported. (E1/E2)
11. **The clinician comparison** uses 188 states from turns 2-4 chosen where the chatbot's disposition changed, one written question per state from a truncated transcript; perseveration is defined as sharing at least one question category with the preceding question, and 61.9% of chatbot questions carry more than one code. (E1/E2)
12. **Funding and conflicts** are stated: four authors are employees of a company that makes consumer assistants, none of its assistants is tested, and that company funded the clinician ratings. Data and code are promised at publication. (E1)

## What holds
A branching design that keeps earlier dispositions out of the context; a matched single-message counterfactual with information fidelity checked by a second model family; confirmation analyses on clinician-consensus labels; per-endpoint and per-case reporting; a context-source ablation; decoding settings, access dates and token counts; a plain statement that the results "do not establish how often such failures occur or cause harm in real-world use".

## Scorecard against the predictions
8 of 13 held (LLM-simulated user; wide heterogeneity; clinician comparison on fixed transcripts; mitigations untested; coupling inferred from association; over-triage discussed; data promised at publication; causal title over a format-confounded contrast), 3 partial (acknowledgement of the format confound; clustering; whether an author-affiliated developer's assistant is tested), 2 missed (the 56% fraction is not stated; a forced provisional disposition is not what creates the anchor, because the design avoids it). Detail in `predictions_fixed_2026-11-09.md`.

## Limitations: what this does and does not establish
- One reader; the flags are leads tied to a table or section, not verdicts; the supplement figures were read through captions.
- It establishes what the paper's design and tables support. It does not establish that chatbots do not anchor, that the effect is absent in real conversations, or anything about deployed products.
- The number of flags is not a severity ranking (see the series overview).
