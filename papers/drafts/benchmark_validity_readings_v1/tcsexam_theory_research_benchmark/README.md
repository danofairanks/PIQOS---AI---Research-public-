# A Compact Suite Checked Against Its Own Data: Reading TCSExam

*AI-assisted. Living research: staged, not peer reviewed, may be revised or withdrawn.*

**Scope of naming.** One paper, "TCSExam: Evaluating Frontier LLMs on the Full Range of TCS Research" (arXiv:2611.07962v1, 5 November 2026, 52 pp.; the paper names Tsinghua University, Moonshot AI and the Shanghai Qizhi Institute). The authors are called "the authors"; people named in the works it cites are not named here. No intent is attributed to anyone.

**Disclosure.** AI-assisted (Claude, working under this project's own non-default standard: the operator's instructions, preregistration and primary-source checking discipline; direction, selection and release decisions are the operator's); single reader. Two framework conditions apply. One author affiliation is the developer of the Kimi models that the benchmark evaluates, and the benchmark repository is under that organisation's name. A model from the assisting model's developer (Opus 5.5) is evaluated, on the smaller suite only, and the Claude Code harness is also used for four other agents. Scored against the text only. Read from an operator-supplied copy (PDF sha256 beginning 9ad7212720907bd5): the abstract, Sections 1 to 6, Tables 1 to 3 and Figures 1 to 5, and Appendices A, B.2, C, D, E, F and H in the parts that bear on the flags below. The repository, the evaluation manifest and the per-task tables beyond those cited were not read; nothing was re-run. Reading lens: the series overview plus the five questions added on 2026-11-09. Predictions were fixed before the body, and again before Appendix A, was read (`predictions_fixed_2026-11-10.md`).

## What it is
A benchmark of 100 theoretical-computer-science research tasks in four tracks: 20 theorems to prove in Lean 4, 30 solver-engineering problems, 20 constructions scored against documented records, and 30 open conjectures to prove or refute. Every submission is checked by the Lean kernel or an executable checker. The full suite (eight agents) and a 20-task Lite suite (nine agents) are summarised by an Index, I = 30P + 30S + 30C + 10O, over the four track means. Headline: GPT-6 Astra leads the full suite (71.9), Opus 5.5 leads Lite (72.3), and the two suites rank the eight shared agents alike (Spearman 0.93; 26 of 28 pairs), "supporting Lite as a compact, discriminative benchmark". Astra proves 14 of 20 theorems in one attempt each; the open-conjecture track yields "independently verified special cases and bounds".

## Flags (ranked; post hoc)
1. **Lite is not an independent check of the full suite.** Fifteen of Lite's twenty tasks "reuse the same submissions" as the full suite for the eight shared agents (Appendix H.2); the Lite list was chosen by comparing subsets on how well they "preserved pairwise agent orderings and approximated Full track scores" for the original six agents, and was fixed before two later agents ran. Of the 28 agent pairs, 15 are among the six agents used for selection and all 15 agree; the 13 pairs involving a later agent agree in 11 (both reversals involve one agent). So the headline agreement is largely in-sample and partly mechanical; the out-of-sample evidence is 13 pairs and two agents. (C2; E1/E2)
2. **The solver term is referenced to a model of an author's organisation, and the choice changes the order.** The Index uses a "Kimi K3-relative" score: each instance outcome is standardised against the mean and sample standard deviation of three Kimi K3 attempts, passed through a Gaussian CDF, so the scale is in units of that model's own run-to-run variation; 32 of 1,870 instances are dropped for lacking two valid reference outcomes. The authors justify it by one oversized circuit that depressed a competitor's mean under the original score. The two scores order the five non-leading original agents differently (spread 5.4 points relative, 16.4 original), and recomputing the Index with the original score reorders the middle of the field. Both scores are reported (Table 16), which is good practice; the Index uses the first. The Index itself is "a later reporting summary". (C8; E1/E2)
3. **The theorem track is conditioned on a screening agent, as the authors say.** "Over 40 theorem problems were retired after being solved in pilot evaluations", and Appendix C.3 states that the collection "is conditioned on the screening agent's outcomes, which can affect its comparison with other agents". The screening model is recorded in the authors' evaluation records but is not named in the text read. A track filtered against one model family favours models that were not used for screening. (E1)
4. **The open-conjecture track measures retrieval and adaptation here, not resolution.** Network access was allowed on this track; the accepted results (one of three attempts on one task for one agent; two of three and three of three on two tasks for another) "adapt these existing results" from published counterexamples and Lean proofs that were released after the tasks were set ("The tasks were formulated before these OpenAI results were released"). The other 28 tasks have no accepted solution. The authors suggest evaluating agents "without Internet access on conjectures still open at the time of testing". The abstract's wording is about partial results. (C7; E1)
5. **The top of Lite is one task and the middle of Full is unresolved.** Opus 5.5's 5.3-point Lite lead over Astra is one theorem task (6.0 points) minus 0.75 on the solver term; 4 of 5 (95% Wilson 37.6-96.4%) against 3 of 5 (23.1-88.2%). On the full suite ranks 3 to 6 sit within 2 points (46.8, 46.0, 45.5, 44.8) and reorder under equal weights, no open-conjecture term, or single-track weights; ranks 1 and 2 are stable. A theorem-free agent still earns about 40 of the leader's 72 points from the construction and solver terms. (C3, C4, C5; E2)
6. **Attempts, harnesses and resources are not matched.** Five agents have one attempt per task and four have three; harnesses differ (Codex, Kimi Code, Claude Code, Grok Build); the shared budget is a 12-hour wall-clock cap. Per theorem attempt, mean input tokens range from 6.5 million (the leader) to 253 million, output tokens from 91 thousand to 1.6 million, and time from 0.68 to 9.3 hours (ratios 38.9, 17.6 and 13.7). The leader used the least, so the ranking is not simply a compute ranking; token accounting differs by harness, so the figures are not like-for-like. Intervals are not given; Astra's 14 of 20 has a 95% Wilson interval of 48.1-85.5%. (C2 in `checks_appendixA.py`, C6; E1/E2)
7. **Contamination is uncontrolled and cannot be tested from the paper.** No model training cutoffs are stated anywhere in the text. Theorem sources are 3 papers from 2023, 6 from 2024, 5 from 2025 and 6 from 2026; the leading agent solved all six 2026-sourced theorems and 8 of 14 earlier ones (a weak pattern at n = 20, one-sided probability 0.077; opposite to a simple older-is-easier reading, which does not rule contamination in or out). Solver and construction tasks rest on long-public libraries and records (safeguards are hidden instances and structure-preserving transformations, with no novelty claim); the conjecture track allowed retrieval. (checks_appendixA.py; E1/E2)
8. **Validity of tasks and of new bounds rests on the authors' own agent-assisted review.** Statement fidelity is checked by agents under expert sign-off, with some re-translation passes by the authoring agent itself; no count of mis-formalisations found or fixed appears in the text read; kernel acceptance proves the formal statement, not that it matches the research claim. The improved constructions and an upper bound reported as improving a published one are verified with archived verifiers and a source comparison; no external confirmation is in the text read. (E1/E3)

## What holds
Every output is checked by the Lean kernel or an executable checker; the Index is shown beside its four track means; the solver score is reported under both metrics with failure counts; per-task tables, raw outcomes, versioned tasks and retirement reasons are recorded; the Lite list was fixed before two agents ran; the screening bias and the use of published counterexamples are stated by the authors; recomputing the Index from the track means reproduces it (largest difference 0.07; 0.04 on Lite) and the 0.93 and 26-of-28 figures reproduce from the Index values; Docker recipes and judging code are released.

## Scorecard against the predictions
Part 1: 12 of 12 held, 4 in part. Part 2 (Appendix A): 5 of 5 held, 1 in part. Several predictions were generous toward finding weakness; the paper's own statements of the screening bias and of retrieval on the conjecture track are where it was most open. Detail in `predictions_fixed_2026-11-10.md`.

## Evidence tier and deeper-pass flag
**Tier:** E1 executed (the Index, rank correlation, pair counts, weighting schemes, intervals and Table 16 reordering in `checks.py`; source years and resource ratios in `checks_appendixA.py`); E2 read (main text and the cited appendices); E3 interpretation (flag 8's reading of agent-assisted review; any external confirmation of the new bounds).
**Deeper pass:** recommended and cheap: a task-level bootstrap of the Index from the released per-task records; the Lite-versus-Full agreement recomputed on the two out-of-sample agents with additional ones; the screening model named and the benchmark re-scored with it held out; the full suite re-run for conjectures with no network; model cutoffs stated and tasks dated after each; a count of mis-formalisations found in review. Not done.

## Limitations: what this does and does not establish
- One reader; the flags are leads tied to a page, table or appendix, not verdicts, and the paper's repository may answer them in places not read.
- It establishes what the paper's tables and text support about the Index, the two suites and the evaluation conditions. It does not establish that any agent's ranking is wrong, only how much of the evidence is in-sample, reference-dependent or unresolved.
- The number of flags is not a severity ranking (see the series overview).
