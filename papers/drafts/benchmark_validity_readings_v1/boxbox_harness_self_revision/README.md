## What it is
PerfRace is a benchmark of 100 performance-optimisation tasks built from public repositories with a fixed workload each, scored by best-so-far correct speedup in a shadow container (50 tasks for testing; 40 train and 10 validation for the offline baseline). BoxBox is an agent harness in which the task-solving agent calls `boxbox()` to pause, review its own trajectory, edit the full harness, and hot-reload it with context preserved. The model is fixed ("fixed language model M", Section 4.1); the harness changes during one task. Result on 50 test tasks, three four-hour runs each: Speedup@4h 1.702 (BoxBox), 1.539 (Harness Scaling, an external controller revising the harness between attempts), 1.442 (frozen harness), 1.281 (offline Harness Evolution); mean model cost $16.43, $12.98, $23.22, $24.56; "Speedup/$" 0.4886, 0.2872, 0.2142, 0.1243. The abstract says the method achieves "more efficient use of task time and model cost".

## Flags (ranked; post hoc)
1. **The per-dollar column is a mean of per-task ratios, and the BoxBox lead reverses with the metric.** Reported: 0.4886 against 0.2872 (x1.70). On the ratio of the table's own means: 0.1036 against 0.1186 (x0.87, Harness Scaling higher). Gain per dollar, (S-1)/cost: 0.0427 against 0.0415 (x1.03, a tie). The reported column sits 4.7 times (BoxBox) and 2.4 times (Harness Scaling) above the ratio of means, so cheap runs dominate it, and a run that does nothing earns a speedup of 1 per its cost. The text concedes "Although HS has a lower total model cost" and rests the claim on "our task-level metric"; the separate curve of speedup against cumulative cost (Figure 1b) is the only view that favours BoxBox without that choice, and it was read from the plot only. (C1; E1 arithmetic, E3 plot)
2. **The headline lead has no task-level interval.** BoxBox exceeds the strongest baseline by 0.163 (10.6%) and the frozen harness by 0.260 (18.0%). The bands in Figure 1 are described as "fixed-task run-resampling" intervals: three runs per task are resampled over "the fixed 50-task set", so task sampling is not in them, and the centre line (geometric then harmonic mean) is a different statistic from the band's. The per-task curves (Figures 11-15) are mixed: by eye BoxBox is best or tied-best on about 24 of 50 tasks against 25% by chance among four methods, with large wins on a few tasks (about 100x on two) and clear losses elsewhere (about 53x for the frozen harness against about 15x on one). The authors chose the harmonic mean "to limit the influence of extreme values". No win-rate table or paired test is given. (C2; E1 for the text, E3 for the tally)
3. **The ablation does not isolate agent triggering, and it is small.** Fourteen sampled test tasks, no intervals, no frozen-harness reference on the same 14 (full BoxBox there scores 1.584 against 1.702 on all 50). "Agent-triggered" reviews carry "a 30-minute cooldown after each completed review", so at most eight fit in four hours (median observed five); the compared variant reviews every 30 minutes. The speedup difference is 5.0% (1.584 against 1.508). Against Harness Scaling, BoxBox differs in two ways at once: the agent decides when to review, and context is preserved where Harness Scaling resets it each attempt; the paper itself says resets lower input cost and lose earlier findings, but no run separates the two. The per-dollar column in Table 2 again sits 1.4 to 3.3 times above the ratio of means. (C3; E1/E2)
4. **The score is a running maximum over noisy timings.** Best-so-far speedup takes the largest measured value across submissions. The paper reports baseline coefficients of variation under 10% on every wall-clock task (84 of 99 under 6%), and its own case study shows a change that looked 9.4% faster in separate batches and made no difference when runs were alternated. No re-measurement of the winning snapshot and no per-method submission counts are reported, so how much of each method's value is the maximum of noise, and whether that differs by method, is not shown. (E1)
5. **"Recursive" is claimed but not tested as recursion, and the improved object is the target code, not the agent.** Reviews run under the revised harness, and the paper's Appendix A.4 traces helpers being extended across reviews (the compiler task). No result shows gains compounding across reviews, none runs a revised harness on fresh tasks, and the offline baseline's result runs the other way (revised harnesses did not transfer). The supported claim is in-task adaptation of a harness by a fixed model; the closing line, "supporting harness optimization as part of an agent's own capabilities", is wider than the ablations. (E1/E2)
6. **Both baselines are the authors' adaptations, with design choices that disadvantage them.** The offline baseline trains on 45-minute runs, validates at 2 h and is tested at 4 h; one traced test failure comes from a harness check that assumed the wrong repository path ("473 blocked submission requests" over about 3 h 46 min). That shows a flaw in one revision, not that offline harness evolution cannot work. Harness Scaling resets context each attempt (median 12 attempts). Neither is given the advantage the method has. (E1)
7. **Builders, benchmark, baselines and filter are one group, and one model plays several roles.** The same model that runs the agent and the review also judged "optimization potential" and "evaluable process" for the candidate pool that became the test set (239 of 103,475 reached human curation; 93,992 failed on stars alone). One model, one initial harness, one VM type. The word "contamination" does not occur; the test projects are public repositories and the model's training cutoff is not stated. (E1)
8. **Small loose ends.** Appendix B.5 evaluates 88 of the 100 tasks with no stated reason; Table 3 lists GPT-5.6-Terra rates and no use of it appears in the text read; Figure 1 cost labels differ from Table 1 by $0.01-0.02; the paper says there are "1,983 boundaries" where 2,132 attempts minus 150 runs is 1,982. None changes a conclusion. (E1/E2)

## What holds
The negative result for the offline baseline is reported with its costs; the paper says the per-dollar claim is "under our task-level metric"; the benchmark is built workload-first with agent-human verification (mean 9.1 hours and 12 interactions per task), shadow-container verification, a stated red-team pass on all 100 tasks with a caught cheat, and per-task measurement variation reported; Table 4's cost lines reproduce (150 runs at $24.56 is $3,684 against $3,683.34; $33.73 per run with offline costs); the frozen harness scores 1.442 on the test tasks and 1.435 on 88 tasks, a good internal check; the security paragraph states the system is "a research prototype" and not a security boundary; the paper gives the context-reset trade-off for Harness Scaling itself.

## Limitations: what this does and does not establish
- One reader; the flags are leads tied to a page, table or figure, not verdicts, and the paper may answer them in places not read or in released material.
- It establishes what the paper's tables and text support. It does not establish that BoxBox fails to beat the baselines, only that the headline per-dollar number and the interval do not carry the reading placed on them; the matched-time speedup lead is real in the table and its size is uncertain.
- The number of flags is not a severity ranking (see the series overview).
