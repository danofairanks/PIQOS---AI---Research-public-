# Proving Which Process Ran: Reading Six Provenance Sources for the Distance Between the Statement and the Headline

*AI-assisted. Living research: staged, not peer reviewed, may be revised or withdrawn.*

**Scope of naming.** Six sources read together as one genre (proofs and attestations of which process produced an output): a report on provable inference of a large language model (November 2026); zkLLM (arXiv 2404.16109v1, CCS 2024); zkGPT (USENIX Security 2025); Artemis (arXiv 2409.12055v2); arXiv 2511.02620v1 (verification of model inference against exfiltration; statistical, not a proof system); and the C2PA Specification 2.2 (media provenance). A seventh, zkCNN (CCS 2021), is not read (section "Not read"). Authors are called "the authors" and are not named; organisations are named only as each source names them. No intent is attributed to anyone.

**Disclosure.** AI-assisted (Claude, working under this project's own non-default standard: the operator's instructions, preregistration and primary-source checking discipline; direction, selection and release decisions are the operator's); single reader. The report and arXiv 2511.02620 have authors affiliated with the assisting model's developer; they are scored on their text only. Predictions were fixed before the fuller reads (`predictions_fixed_2026-11-11.md`).

## What it is
Not a reading of one paper. It asks whether a class of systems that certify which model or process produced an output shares the failure modes recorded in this series. Each source is read for the statement it proves, the words in its title and abstract, the performance claim and its baseline, the limits and where they are stated, how it compares with prior systems, who evaluates, and whether any case where the certified process is wrong or harmful is tested.

## Flags (ranked; post hoc)
1. **zkGPT's abstract speedups are the two largest ratios in its own Table 3, and the slower-than-a-comparator result is in the body.** The abstract reports "nearly 279× and 185× speedup" over "state-of-the-art systems such as" two named systems. Table 3's printed prover times give 6096 s / 21.8 s = 279.6 and 4026 s / 21.8 = 184.7. The same table gives 112.3 s for a VOLE-based system (ratio 5.15, stated in the body as 5.1×) and 15.8 s for zkLLM on an A100 (ratio 0.72); the body says "though our concrete prover performance is 30% slower" and attributes it to hardware (printed times give 1.38 times the time). The figure for the first compared system is "summed" from its paper rather than run, by a footnote. The body is open about all of this; the abstract does not carry it. (E1: `checks.py`; pattern 19)
2. **Motivation words are larger than the statement proved, in three of six abstracts, and the body narrows each.** zkLLM's abstract frames "the authenticity of outputs generated by LLMs" and "AI legislation"; the statement is correct execution of a committed model, with a semi-honest verifier stated in the setting section ("A semi-honest assumption is applied to the verifier"). Artemis opens its abstract with "trust, accountability, and compliance"; its statement is the cost of checking the commitment, and its introduction says a proof of correct inference "is, by itself, not generally useful in practice". arXiv 2511.02620's abstract says its framework "can provably mitigate steganographic exfiltration" and specifies "minimal trust assumptions"; the body assumes tamper-evident logs. zkGPT, the report and C2PA do not show this. (E1/E2; pattern 20)
3. **No proof system tests its proof on a wrong or substituted process and reports what the proof then catches.** zkGPT's introduction motivates detecting a provider that, in its abstract's words, "may deploy smaller models to reduce costs" and the report motivates the same; neither evaluation read contains a case where a smaller or altered model is run and the verifier's response is measured. arXiv 2511.02620 is the exception in kind: it evaluates adversarial exfiltration with a measured false-positive rate, but it is statistical verification, not a proof. C2PA describes an ongoing harms-and-misuse process; no worked wrong-signer case was read. Accountability is therefore stated as motivation, not measured as a property. (E2/E3; pattern 21)
4. **Performance claims are single configurations against authors' baselines; two include estimates.** No standard deviations, intervals or repeated-run statistics turned up in zkLLM or zkGPT by search (E3 for figures). zkLLM's comparison figure says results of test cases with out-of-memory errors "are estimated and marked in red", and its text reports "an approximate 50x speedup" at sizes the comparator could not run; whether the 50× rests on the estimated points was not established. zkGPT's comparison mixes a 16-core CPU with a GPU system and one number is not run. Artemis compares against a strawman built from the earlier construction. (E1 for quotes; E3 for the 50×)
5. **Conditions that cap the claim sit in the body, not the abstract.** zkLLM: semi-honest verifier, a 2^16 scaling factor, 2048-token samples, an output error of about 1e-2 reported as comparable to half-precision rounding. zkGPT: quantisation level 16, GPT-2 only, a BN254 curve at about 100-bit security. Artemis: a single-column witness restriction. The report states its limits in Section 7 and an appendix: an integer model under greedy decoding, one GPU, AI-generated and unreviewed protocol description and prover/verifier code not proved equivalent to the formal specification, zero knowledge proved formally only for requests proved one at a time. (E1; pattern 13)
6. **The evaluator is inside the builders' loop in every machine-learning source.** Each benchmarks its own system. The report and arXiv 2511.02620 share a developer with the assisting model; this reader's scoring is on text only, and the same-training-distribution caution applies. C2PA is a specification with no benchmark. (E1)

## What holds
The genre is more careful about its statement than the benchmark series: two sources volunteer what a bare proof does not establish (Artemis; the report), and C2PA says that its specifications "SHOULD NOT provide value judgments about whether a given set of provenance data is 'good' or 'bad'". The report is the only source that states the post-hoc limit directly: "Post-hoc proving does not prevent users from being served incorrect responses, it only ensures tampering will be detected." Accuracy cost of quantisation is measured by zkLLM (perplexity increase below 0.1) and zkGPT (Table 4). zkGPT's body discloses the comparator results its abstract omits.

## Evidence tier and deeper-pass flag
**Tier:** E1 executed (arithmetic on printed numbers, `checks.py`); E2 read (abstracts, statement sections, evaluations and limits of the sources named above; not full texts: appendices and proofs by search or not at all); E3 interpretation (flags 3 and 4; the absence of a control case and of intervals is a finding about the text read).
**Deeper pass:** recommended and cheap for flag 3: for each proof system, check the released code or the evaluation for a negative test (a substituted or altered model, a tampered output) and what the verifier does; for flag 4, whether zkLLM's 50× uses the estimated points. Neither done. Reading zkCNN would add the earliest member of the genre.

## Not read
zkCNN (CCS 2021; ePrint 2021/673 and the ACM page returned bot-challenge pages, not bypassed). The sources were read in part; the report's companion blog post and long protocol description were not read.

## Limitations: what this does and does not establish
- Six sources chosen by what the reader had to hand, not sampled; nothing here is a rate for the genre.
- One reader; the flags are leads tied to a place in each text, not verdicts, and the sources may answer them where not read.
- It establishes what the texts say they prove and how they report performance. It does not establish that any proof system is unsound, or that any speed claim is wrong; the zkGPT flag is a statement about which ratios the abstract names.
- This is a first pass at the series' open question on post-hoc assurance (section 8 of the overview); the seed set here is proofs and attestations, a subset of that question, and the control set the overview asks for is not built.
