# Numbers Copied, Claims Written: Reading Four Manuscripts from an Automated Paper-Writing Pipeline

*AI-assisted. Living research: staged, not peer reviewed, may be revised or withdrawn.*

**Scope of naming.** One system: PaperOrchestra, an open-source multi-agent pipeline for writing research papers (code repository; arXiv:2604.05018v1, 6 Apr 2026, 65 pp.; the author block names Google Cloud AI Research). The authors are called "the authors". The object examined is four full-length manuscripts the project page shows as its generated-manuscript gallery, plus what the repository and the paper say about how they were made. The two real papers the manuscripts were regenerated from are described by role (a CVPR 2025 paper on certified robustness for human trajectory prediction; an ICLR 2025 paper on a generalist model for combinatorial optimization), and works they cite are described by role.

**Disclosure.** AI-assisted (Claude, working under this project's own non-default standard; direction, selection and release decisions are the operator's); single reader. The paper reports judge models from two developers; none is from the assisting model's developer, and no model from that developer appears in the text read. The manuscripts were read as page images from the project page (OCR, with transcription errors); the repository was read as a static copy and nothing from it was run.

## What it is
A pipeline of five agents (outline, literature review, section writing, content refinement, plotting) that turns an "idea" file and an "experimental log" into a LaTeX manuscript for a conference template. The repository README says the benchmark dataset "is **not included** in this code repository" and will be released separately; it ships one input example and no generated manuscripts. The paper evaluates against baseline writers with LLM judges and human side-by-side comparisons and reports "absolute win rate margin of 50%–68% in literature review quality, and 14%–38% in overall manuscript quality"; that evaluation was not examined here. Its appendix E.1 says the displayed manuscripts are generated from the raw materials of one CVPR and one ICLR paper "under the sparse idea setting", each shown with plots off and plots on: four manuscripts, two source papers.

## Flags (ranked; post hoc; leads, not verdicts)
1. **The source paper reappears in the manuscript's own literature.** The trajectory-style manuscript (plots off) cites the real source paper in its text as earlier work ("Initial efforts to establish certified robustness for human trajectories", with the source as reference [1]) while its abstract claims "the first mathematically certified robustness framework"; the plots-on run lists the source in its references and claims "the first certified robustness framework"; the optimisation-style plots-off run lists the source paper itself, under the manuscript's own title, in its references (the plots-on run does not). Mechanism (E2, from the paper and a static read of the code): the literature agent filters by date only (research cutoffs of November 2024 for the CVPR paper and October 2024 for the ICLR paper); both source preprints are earlier (arXiv listings: 20 March 2024 and 21 June 2024); the code has no rule excluding the target paper; the writer is told that "at least 90% of the gathered literature pool must be actively integrated and cited". The paper's anti-leakage measures are anonymised inputs ("stripping authors and titles") and a prompt; its limitations paragraph names "training data contamination". Retrieval of the target's own preprint is not among them. (E1 for the reference lists and dates; E2 for the mechanism.)
2. **Abstract strength is a property of the draw.** Same inputs, two runs, optimisation-style: one abstract says the model "matches or exceeds the performance of specialized models"; the other says "highly competitive with specialized" baselines; the source says "only slightly inferior to the specialized baselines". The first manuscript's own Table 1 has the multi-task model worse than its single-task model in 8 of 8 cells (mean 0.71 gap points; ATSP 0.91% against 0.30%) and its Sec. 4.2 says multi-task "sacrifices minimal performance". (E1)
3. **A headline drops its condition, in both runs.** "can be fine-tuned onto eight entirely unseen tasks in under 3.5 minutes": the body says this holds for "supervised fine-tuning using only 128 labeled instances"; the plots-off text gives "a budget of 5.5 hours" for the unsupervised route and the plots-on text omits it. The source says "a few minutes" with few-shot supervision and "a couple of hours" without. The specific 3.5 minutes and 5.5 hours are not in the source's text (E3: they may be in its figures). (E1/E2)
4. **Mechanism text drifts between runs and from the source; a theorem and constants appear in one run only.** The source describes a multi-step diffusion denoiser and writes "We are the first to propose an unconditional diffusion-based denoiser"; its 11-page main text has no theorem, lemma or proof (0 hits). The plots-off manuscript is titled "Diffusion-Denoised ..." and describes "an efficient single-step DAE" that avoids "multi-step diffusion sampling", fixes the output clamp at "50 meters" and states a "Theorem 1" whose index rule contains no confidence level although the statement says "with confidence level" 1-alpha (E2: no sampling-error correction is visible in the stated indices; no proof is given; the multi-modal extension is asserted without a theorem). The plots-on manuscript uses "diffusion" 28 times and has no theorem. (E1 for the text differences; E2 for the mathematics.)
5. **Citations exist; relevance is not checked.** In the two reference lists checked, 12 of 12 arXiv-identified references resolve at arXiv with matching titles (two have name glitches). One relevance defect (E2): a driving-motion-forecasting transformer is cited in the optimisation manuscript's introduction as a foundation for deep-learning routing solvers ("sequential trajectory generation"), consistent with the citation quota in flag 1. (E1/E2)
6. **No limitations section and no disclosure inside any manuscript,** although the pipeline paper has a limitations appendix and says the system is meant as "an advanced assistive tool rather than a fully independent writing entity", and the repository calls its output "submission-ready". The outline agent's prompts ask for a limitation hypothesis about competing methods, not the authors' own; the template guidelines mark ethics and reproducibility statements optional. (E1 for the manuscripts; E2 for the prompts.)
7. **Two runs per venue, one draw each, with the selection of the displayed samples not described in the lines read.** The pairs show large differences (word-sequence similarity 0.27 to 0.29; decimal-token overlap 0.69 and 0.86) but are not an interval. (E2)
8. **Not examined, and why it matters for reading the paper's headline.** The paper's quality claims rest on LLM judges and human side-by-side comparisons whose criteria include scientific soundness and depth. Whether those judgements track agreement between a manuscript's claims and its own evidence, which flags 2 to 4 show varying between runs, is untested here. (E3)

## What holds
Numbers are copied from the input log and stay faithful: 51 of 53 decimal tokens in the trajectory-style plots-off manuscript are in the source text (the other two are a rounding form and a derived difference), 120 of 125 and 105 of 111 in the optimisation-style manuscripts (the rest are OCR splits and section numbers); every row of the trajectory-style Table 1 and the numbers in Tables 2 to 6 equal the source's. Citations resolve. The paper states its limitations and its assistive intent. The run-to-run differences above are about wording and mechanism, not about the results the log supplies.

## Tools
`tool_scan_summary.json` (counts only). paper_rigor: one structural gap in all four (no limitations section). verification_lint: 7 to 8 gaps on the trajectory-style manuscripts and 157 to 160 on the optimisation-style (table cells counted as uncited statistics); all four lack a disclaimer section (the tool's severity counts are 1, 7, 160 and 157, mostly numbers inside tables); the flagged numbers are the manuscripts' own results or OCR date artefacts. attractor_scan: weak flags only (a venue name containing "pattern recognition", "safety" meaning driving safety, "core reasoning process" meaning the architecture, an ordinary future-work sentence). Every flag was read; what survives is the two structural facts in flag 6.

## Evidence tier and deeper-pass flag
**Tier:** E1 executed (decimal-token comparison, table comparison, reference resolution at arXiv, text and phrase counts); E2 read (the four manuscripts as page images, the repository's prompts and templates, the pipeline paper's appendices A, B, D and E.1); E3 interpretation (flags 1's mechanism, 3's missing figures, 8).
**Deeper pass:** recommended if the dataset is released: run the pipeline on inputs for which the target preprint is excluded from retrieval, and score abstract claims against body evidence across repeated runs. Not done.

## Limitations: what this does and does not establish
- One reader; four manuscripts, two source papers, one draw per setting; the displayed samples may not be representative of the pipeline's output and how they were chosen was not found in the lines read.
- OCR transcription of page images; quotations were checked against the OCR text or the source text, and numbers were compared as decimal tokens, which cannot catch a changed integer or a changed comparison.
- The source papers' supplementary pages were not read; a theorem or a constant might sit there (flag 4 is about the main text).
- It establishes what the manuscripts, the paper and the code support, and that results are copied faithfully while claims and mechanisms vary. It does not establish how often this happens, that the pipeline is worse than other writers, or anything about the paper's own judged comparisons. No pipeline code was run.
