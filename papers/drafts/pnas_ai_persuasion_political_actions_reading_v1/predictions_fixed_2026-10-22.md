# PREREG: "AI can persuade people to take political actions" (PNAS 2026, 123:e2611368123, published 2026-10-16; operator upload, 9 pp, sha256 fbcb1aae409d4700…). Written 2026-10-22 AFTER seeing the title, the first-page metadata, the Significance statement and the first three sentences of the abstract (printed by the extraction command), and BEFORE reading anything else. Claims are about what the body will show. E1 executed / E2 read / E3 interpretation. AI-assisted.
Seen already: two preregistered experiments, N = 17,950 responses from 14,779 people, conversational AI, outcomes include signing real petitions and donating to charity; "AI conversations meaningfully shifted behavior"; "conditions that influenced attitudes were not the same ones that influenced behavior"; an information-provision strategy "was not associated with greater behavioral change than other strategies".
- P1. Behavioral effects are small in absolute terms (≤ 5 percentage points on petition signing vs control) while the text calls them meaningful.
- P2. The information-provision result is a non-significant contrast, not an equivalence test; "not associated with greater" is absence of evidence.
- P3. "Attitude predictors differ from behavior predictors" rests partly on comparing significant vs non-significant effects across outcomes rather than a direct interaction test.
- P4. The control is a no-conversation or non-persuasive-chat baseline; there is no static-message or human-persuader comparator at the behavioral stage, so "AI persuades" is not "AI persuades more than alternatives".
- P5. Actions are low-cost (a click on a real petition; a small donation from study money or a bonus), and the limitations say so, while the title does not.
- P6. 17,950 responses from 14,779 people means repeat participation; clustering or dedup is handled in the analysis (checkable), and attrition or post-treatment selection is discussed.
- P7. Participants come from an opt-in online panel; behavior is measured within the same session or a short follow-up, not later.
- P8. The paper reports effect sizes with intervals and a multiple-comparison correction; at least one headline number is from a model (regression with covariates) not the raw difference.
- P9. The abstract/conclusion contain at least one statement the paper's own tables or figures do not support or report for a different subset (the mismatch definition from the ScientistTwo reading, section 4).
- P10. Data/code/preregistration are linked (OSF or similar) and a Supporting Information file holds the full results (not in the upload).
Misses are recorded as missed, not edited.
