## Abstract

Triggered by a LinkedIn post naming "yanning" — public advocacy volume
overwriting a research direction's original inventor in audience memory —
this paper checks a distinct, adjacent claim the same specimen raised: that
credentialed critics of the LLM-scaling narrative propose alternatives that
are themselves unfalsifiable, capital-driven bets rather than genuine
departures from it. Applying `governance_binding_axiom_v1.md` §4's
three-tier enforcement method directly to Yann LeCun's AMI Labs (world
models / JEPA, which per TechCrunch's March 2026 reporting raised $1.03B
at a $3.5B pre-money valuation)
finds a more mixed picture than a pure Opaque Promise: the venture is
attached to a real, dated, peer-reviewed-adjacent formal result (a May 2026
proof of when JEPA architectures can recover world structure, paired with a
benchmark that found current models "collapse under minor visual shifts" —
a published admission of limitation, not overclaiming) and a hedged, honest
product timeline (LeCun told the BBC industrial applications are "hoped
for" in 2027, with no product likely for roughly five years). The same
method applied to Gary Marcus's neurosymbolic AI advocacy finds a
comparable mix: a third-party-tracked prediction record (Forecast Fools'
public tracker) and specific, already-checked claims about mainstream LLMs
(GPT-5 did not solve hallucination; agents did not turn out reliable), set
against one vaguer core claim — neurosymbolic AI "will make an absolute
fortune" — carrying no stated timeline or metric. Neither figure's central
architectural bet currently carries a hard, dated defeat condition, but
both carry real falsifiable material around that bet, which this paper
treats as a materially different finding than SSI's Opaque Promise, where
none exists at any level. The paper's sharper finding is structural rather
than about either individual's honesty: LeCun's venture reproduces the
scaling narrative's own capital-and-prestige-driven playbook ($1B+ raised
on a credentialed founder's bet before a product exists) even while
proposing a different architecture, and Marcus's own core prediction is
scoped in the same commercial-dominance terms ("an absolute fortune") as
the race he is criticizing — an off-ramp back onto the same expressway,
checked as an asymmetric claim rather than a fully parallel one, since only
LeCun's alternative is currently backed by a comparably-scaled capital bet.

## 1. The method, reused rather than reinvented

`governance_binding_axiom_v1.md` §4 splits enforcement into (a)
zero-cost-but-logged, (b) weak/noisy penalty, (c) true hard constraint, and
proposes checking any claim of the form "X will bind/supersede Y" against
whether a dated, falsifiable defeat condition is attached — a specific
milestone, benchmark, or timeline whose failure would count as evidence
against the claim, not merely evidence the claim needs more time. This
paper applies that same test to two public claims neither addressed to
this project nor originally analyzed by it: "world models will supersede
LLMs" (LeCun/AMI Labs) and "neurosymbolic AI will supplant/outperform pure
LLMs" (Marcus). The test is symmetric by design — both sides of a stated
rivalry are checked the same way, per this project's own standing
discipline against holding one party in a dispute to a rigor standard not
applied to the other (already used for the Padget/Dillon C. and
Padget/Selassie material logged elsewhere in this project's private
companion repository).

## 2. AMI Labs, checked

**The venture, dated.** LeCun left Meta in November 2025 after twelve
years as Chief AI Scientist, incorporated AMI Labs in Paris in December
2025, and the company went public in January 2026. Per TechCrunch, the
seed round closed
in March 2026 at $1.03B, co-led by Cathay Innovation, Greycroft, Hiro
Capital, HV Capital, and Bezos Expeditions, at a $3.5B pre-money
valuation per TechCrunch (one outlet, Latent Space, separately reports a
$4.5B figure per that outlet; this paper
flags rather than resolves
the discrepancy). Backers include NVIDIA, Samsung, Temasek, Toyota
Ventures, Mark Cuban, and Eric Schmidt.

**What is attached to the claim, checked directly rather than assumed
absent.** In May 2026, LeCun and co-authors (reported affiliations
spanning Brown University, Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory, and Mila)
published two preprints: a formal proof of the conditions under which JEPA
architectures can recover real-world structure, and a paired benchmark
finding that current models "collapse under minor visual shifts." This
second result is a self-reported limitation, not a promotional claim — the
research surfaces where the architecture currently fails, which is
evidence of real empirical engagement rather than narrative-only
advocacy. On product timeline, LeCun told the BBC in July 2026 that the
model would be refined through year-end, with industrial applications
"hoped for" in 2027, and has separately indicated there may be no product
"for something like five years."

**The defeat-condition finding, stated precisely.** This does not clear
the bar for a hard, dated defeat condition on the central claim itself —
"hoped for in 2027" is not a falsifiable commitment, and "no product for
five years" describes a timeline, not a test the claim could fail. But it
is a materially different case from SSI's Opaque Promise
(`register_invariance_self_falsification_v1.md`,
`mission_premise_v1.md`), where this project found no checkable claim at
any level. AMI Labs' surrounding research program has already published a
result against its own architecture (models "collapse under minor visual
shifts") and stated a long, hedged timeline rather than an imminent,
unfalsifiable breakthrough. The credentialed-narrative concern the
triggering LinkedIn post raises is real at the level of funding
(described in §4) but does not, on the evidence found here, extend to the
research program's own reporting standards.

## 3. Gary Marcus / neurosymbolic AI, checked the same way

**The claim.** Marcus posted three predictions on June 4, 2026: some form
of AI, "probably" neurosymbolic, will emerge that is more economical and
data/energy-efficient than LLMs and "will make an absolute fortune"; LLMs
themselves will never be that profitable outside chip vendors; and
today's largest AI bets are premature and mostly will not pay off.

**What is attached to the claim.** Unlike most public AI commentators,
Marcus has a standing, third-party prediction tracker (Forecast Fools,
19 predictions tracked at time of writing) — a real, external
falsifiability mechanism most industry advocacy does not carry. Specific,
already-checkable claims from his 2026 review are concrete and dated:
GPT-5 did not solve hallucination, and AI agents did not turn out to be
reliable despite the surrounding hype — both checkable against public
record independent of Marcus's own framing.

**The defeat-condition finding.** The tracked-prediction infrastructure
and the specific, dated LLM critiques are real falsifiable material,
comparable in kind (not scale) to AMI Labs' published brittleness
benchmark. But the core architectural bet itself — "probably
neurosymbolic... will make an absolute fortune" — carries no stated
timeline, no named benchmark, and a hedge ("probably") on the one
specific technical claim being made. This is closer to an unfalsifiable
core wrapped in a falsifiable track record than the reverse.

## 4. The expressway finding — checked as asymmetric, not fully parallel

The operator's own framing, checked directly against what §2–§3 actually
found rather than assumed to hold by symmetry: LeCun and Marcus share a
critique of the same target (LLM scaling as sufficient), and each
proposes an alternative architecture rather than exiting the underlying
race framing — LeCun's own reported prediction is that world models will
**supersede** LLMs, and Marcus's is that neurosymbolic AI will **make an
absolute fortune**, both stated in terms of which paradigm wins the same
commercial AI race, not in terms of a different relationship to AI
development altogether. Neither proposes, in the material checked here,
a fundamentally different route — funding structure, timeline framing,
and "next paradigm" narrative shape are continuous with the expressway
being critiqued, not a genuine off-ramp from it.

**Where the symmetry breaks, stated precisely rather than smoothed over.**
Only LeCun's alternative is currently backed by a comparably-scaled
capital bet — $1.03B raised (per TechCrunch, 2026) before a product exists
is the specific
shape `governance_binding_axiom_v1.md` and this project's SSI material
already flag as structurally risky regardless of the underlying science's
merit. Marcus's neurosymbolic advocacy, per the material found here, is
not attached to a comparable venture; it remains rhetorical and
predictive (Substack, X, conference papers), not a $1B+ bet. The
"off-ramp back onto the same expressway" framing holds at the level of
race-framing and commercial-dominance language for both figures, but the
capital-scale version of the critique — instant funding on a
credentialed name, no defeat condition attached to the bet itself —
currently applies to LeCun specifically and has not been shown to apply
to Marcus in the same form.

## 5. What this does not establish

- Not a claim that JEPA/world models or neurosymbolic AI are wrong,
  unpromising, or scientifically unsound — the published brittleness
  benchmark and the formal JEPA proof are treated here as real, useful
  research; this paper checks claim structure, not scientific merit.
- Not a claim that LeCun or Marcus are acting in bad faith, or that
  either is knowingly running an Opaque Promise — §2 and §3 both find
  real falsifiable material attached to each figure's public claims,
  which is the opposite of what bad-faith unfalsifiability would predict.
- Not a claim that AMI Labs and Marcus's neurosymbolic advocacy are
  equivalent in capital scale, institutional weight, or risk — §4 states
  precisely where the parallel breaks.
- Not an independent re-verification of the $3.5B vs. $4.5B pre-money
  valuation discrepancy between outlets (per TechCrunch vs. per Latent
  Space, respectively) — flagged, not resolved.
- Not a resolution of the "yanning" attribution question (Fukushima /
  Marcus-predates-LeCun-on-the-wall-argument / Schmidhuber) that
  originally triggered this line of inquiry — that is a distinct claim
  about credit attribution, checked separately in this session's prior
  conversation, not re-analyzed in this paper.
- Sourcing tier stated once, at the top: every figure and quotation above
  rests on convergent search-aggregator reporting, not a primary-document
  read; flagged for follow-up verification against AMI Labs' and Marcus's
  own primary statements directly when this project's environment can
  reach them.

## Addendum (2026-08-20) — the triggering source has an undisclosed stake, and LeCun's own public rhetoric has shifted

**Testas is not a disinterested observer of the LeCun/Schmidhuber
attribution question — this should have been flagged before his post was
treated as a neutral flag.** Abdelaziz Testas, whose LinkedIn post naming
"yanning" opened this line of inquiry, is the author of a full book,
*The Battle for World Models: Schmidhuber, LeCun, and the Future of AI*,
which frames Schmidhuber-vs-LeCun credit specifically as its subject.
This is not background color: the dispute is live and mutual. Testas
observed, in an earlier post, that LeCun "said little about [world
models'] origins or predecessors," attributing the omission to his
"long-standing reluctance to credit Jürgen Schmidhuber for foundational
ideas in the area" — and LeCun responded to Testas directly and by name
on LinkedIn: "So far, I've ignored this kind of non-sense. But enough is
enough." Correction to how §§1–4 above should be read: this does not
reopen the specific facts already independently verified in this
session's prior conversation (Fukushima's Neocognitron priority over
LeNet; Schmidhuber's 1990 coinage of "world model," 36 years before this
paper's filing date) — those check out against primary sources
regardless of who is pointing at them. What it corrects is the framing:
the triggering post should have been read from the start as one side of
a named, ongoing, commercially-invested dispute, not as a disinterested
"sloppy journalism" flag, and this paper's own Abstract and References
did not carry that disclosure. It now does.

**LeCun's own public register has shifted since leaving Meta, checked
against dated statements rather than asserted.** His earliest post-
departure framing of the LLM-scaling path is combative and totalizing:
"The path to superintelligence — simply train large language models,
train with more synthetic data, hire thousands of people to 'educate'
your system in post-training, and invent new tricks for reinforcement
learning — I think it's complete nonsense. It simply won't work." His
later 2026 public statements run measurably more hedged, and notably the
hedge now extends past the LLM camp he was attacking: at the India AI
Impact Summit, LeCun warned that AGI itself is "still years away" and
cautioned against "overblown expectations" generally, adding that "we
think of ourselves as being general, but it's simply an illusion" —
philosophical hedging about the achievability and even definability of
AGI as a category, not only a claim that LLMs specifically will fail to
reach it. Paired with §2's product-timeline finding (industrial
applications only "hoped for" in 2027, no product likely for roughly
five years), the pattern is a real, dated shift from an early,
totalizing dismissal of the rival path to a later, broader caution that
now covers his own venture's timeline too — checked here as a
description of his public statements over time, not as a claim about his
private motivations or sincerity, which this paper has no instrument to
measure.
