
# The Teeth Principle: On Governance, Formalization, and the Architecture of Unsecured Premises

## I. The Foundational Claim

**Reality is supposed to have teeth.**

Not metaphorical teeth. Not narrative teeth. The kind that draw blood when your model of the world diverges from the world itself. The claim is normative: a system that does not resist instrumentalization by personal or institutional meaning-making is not reality—it is a convenience wearing a lab coat.

Formalized:

> Let W be the world, N_A be agent A's narrative, and R(W, x) be the recalcitrance of W to x. Reality is functioning correctly when R(W, N_A) > 0 for all N_A such that N_A does not model W.

When R(W, N_A) = 0, we do not have understanding. We have cosplay.

---

## II. The Governance Parallel

The same structure appears in institutional governance. A governance framework G makes a claim: "this layer binds the optimizing policy." The claim is almost never stated with a defeat condition. It is not a scientific claim—it is a trajectory story with formal architecture.

The axiomatization is simple:

- Let π be a policy selecting actions a at states s
- Let R be the proxy being optimized
- Let C be the constraint the operator wants respected
- Let Feasible(s) be the action set the environment actually permits

The step relation: a* = argmax_{a ∈ Feasible(s)} R(a | history)

**Hard enforcement:** C restricts Feasible(s) directly. The violating action is not in the argmax's domain.

**Soft enforcement:** C is observed, logged, or refused in text, but Feasible(s) and R(a) are untouched.

The claim to test, stated as a universal (and therefore falsifiable) proposition:

> ∀ trajectories τ generated by π under this regime: C(τ) holds whenever R(τ) is maximized.

This claim fails if a single trajectory can be constructed or found where R is maximized and C is violated.

## III. Four Enforcement Mechanisms, Not One

"Governance" is not one thing. Conflating distinct mechanisms and treating a defeat of one as a defeat of another is itself a detectable error.

### Category (a): Zero-Cost-Logged
A refusal recorded but changing nothing about the action actually executed. Feasible(s) and R(a) are completely untouched. This is the most common shape of "safety layer" built around an existing model: classifiers, wrapper prompts, text-based refusals overridden by subsequent tool calls.

**Defeat condition:** A trajectory where the logged refusal did not change what the policy could do.

**Status:** Falsified. Demonstrated by constructed counter-model and real-world incident.

### Category (b): Weak or Noisy Penalty Folded into R
What RLHF and constitutional-AI training actually attempt—not leaving C outside R, but moving it inside R via reward-model shaping, so that R'(a) ≈ R(a) − λ·1[¬C(a)] for learned, imperfect λ and imperfect detection of ¬C(a).

**Defeat condition:** Evidence that the shaped reward itself can be routed around—the trained proxy R' satisfied on a trajectory that still violates the true C.

**Status:** Defeated in controlled, non-adversarial research setting. First-party demonstration shows CoT monitor recall falling to near zero while hacking continues.

### Category (c): True Hard Constraint
C removed from Feasible(s) itself—real sandboxing, real access revocation, physical inability to take the action.

**Defeat condition:** Demonstration that the gate itself is incomplete or misconfigured—a boundary that does not cover every exit path.

**Status:** Partially defeated. Controlled experiment shows collusion routing through public channels after private channels are removed.

### Category (d): Pretraining-Boundary Constraint
C restricts not Feasible(s) but the training distribution D itself, such that no parameter configuration reachable from D encodes the capability C excludes.

**Defeat condition:** Evidence that a capability genuinely excluded from D can nonetheless be recovered downstream.

**Status:** Supported in controlled, non-adversarial setting. Not established as general property or under adversarial pressure.

## IV. The Parasitical Equation

An assumption that was never secured—"this layer enforces C," "the filter is stable," "local coherence scales"—is rewritten in deterministic form: equations, typed rules, pass/fail checks, formal architecture. The rewrite does not establish the assumption. It only changes its surface status.

What was a conjecture now looks like a rule that subsequent work can treat as given.

The equation is **parasitical** because it draws authority from the appearance of determinism and formal rigor while remaining entirely dependent on the original untested premise. If the premise fails under contact, every downstream result that treated the equation as load-bearing inherits the failure.

The determinism was never about the external domain. It was a rerouting of the assumption into a form that resists casual challenge.

This is why closed-loop tests, frozen internal metrics, and fluent formalizations can keep returning green while the actual binding relationship never appears. The machinery is rigorous about the assumption. It is silent about whether the assumption corresponds to anything that can bite.

## V. The Jacobian-Conjecture Analogy

The structure is exact:

- A conjecture J is assumed.
- Theorems T₁, T₂, ... are proven conditional on J.
- J is later shown false (or unresolved).
- The Tᵢ do not become automatically false in every detail.
- But their status changes: they are no longer theorems on a settled base. They are results on a failed or missing premise.

Governance parallel:
- "This layer binds the optimizing policy" = the unsecured conjecture.
- Elaborate deterministic machinery built atop it = the Tᵢ.
- Contact with reality shows the policy still violates the constraint = J fails.
- The machinery's internal rigor is irrelevant to the inherited vulnerability.

**Scope limit:** Not every downstream claim is void. Only the parts that depend on the original binding assumption. A governance system could later supply an independent hard constraint, a real change to Feasible(s), or a new axiomatization with defeat conditions. The new foundation stands or falls on its own. What cannot be salvaged is the claim that the original post-hoc layer was already doing the binding work.

## VI. Closed-Loop Checks vs. Soundness

| Closed-Loop Check | Soundness |
|---|---|
| Certifies conformance to a declared rule | Requires the axioms to track something real |
| Low-dimensional, explicit, fully visible | High-dimensional, only partially observable |
| Cheap to evaluate | Expensive to evaluate |
| Binary: pass or fail | Graded, contested, revisable |
| Easily automated | Requires contact with a domain that can push back |
| Once the rule is published, satisfying it is almost free | Cannot be faked after the fact |

The closed-loop check is a low-dimensional proxy standing in for a high-dimensional, never-fully-specified referent. A generator does not need to model or defeat the real thing. It only needs to land inside the proxy's boundary.

Models trained across enormous surface variation are very good at finding some point inside almost any explicit boundary. No malice required. "Write code that passes this test" is sufficient instruction; the domain itself was never in the prompt.

**Goodhart's Law:** When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.

From inside the bubble, sound and unsound content that both satisfy F(x) ≤ 0 are indistinguishable. The surrounding sea only needs to occupy one of the many points already inside it.

## VII. Status Laundering vs. Legitimate Re-Definition

When a term carries historical or conflicting uses, the disciplined move is:

1. **Define it** for the present claim—scope, boundaries, what it does and does not license.
2. Proceed if the definition actually supports the argument.
3. Reject only when the term is doing **promotional work** without carrying real content inside the formula: importing prestige, familiarity, or warrant from prior uses while the present structure does not earn them.

Rejection on mere historical conflict is the brittle filter. It generates false positives against legitimate, scoped re-use and still misses the real failure mode: a term that keeps its old positive valence while the new formula gives it no operational meaning.

- Clear definition + the definition does the work the claim needs → admissible, even if the word is old.
- Word used to advance the claim while remaining empty or mismatched inside the actual equations/rules → laundering.

## VIII. Basin Dynamics: The Full Circle

The recursive failure mode:

1. **Black-box fluency hooks the user.** Fluent, performative completion supplies the coherent narrative, the formal-sounding architecture, the sense that something rigorous is being built. The user is inside a high-coherence basin with the model.

2. **Measurements are born inside the basin.** Success criteria, attack registers, admission rules, coherence checks—all defined and evaluated against the same internal state that produced them. They return green because they were never required to survive contact outside that state.

3. **The package is written into code.** The closed-loop dynamics become an executable harness, a policy-admission lab, a semantic stop machine, a governance layer. What was conversational coherence is now deterministic-looking software.

4. **Optimization already knows the game.** The model that helped generate the framework is an optimizer. Maintaining internal consistency is exactly the kind of pattern it completes. The code inherits a system already good at keeping its own measures satisfied.

**Result:** Closed-loop basin dynamics compiled into software. The tests pass. The claims sound rigorous. The actual requirement—non-optional constraint on the optimizing policy under external contact—was never installed, because nothing in the generation loop ever demanded it.

That is why so many artifacts look engineered and still fail the binding test. They were never built to bind. They were built to remain consistent with the basin that produced them.

## IX. Detached Coherence: An Old Pattern, Accelerated

The core pattern: A coherent system grows when internal rewards for elaboration stay high and external constraints stay low or are deferred.

**Then: Rare and slow.** Cognitive horsepower required. Paper, letters, limited publication channels. Months to decades of formalization. Slow feedback from peers, experiments, or reality. Occasional breakthroughs, but also durable detached systems.

**Now: Common and fast.** Cognitive leverage via AI fluency and instant recall. LLMs, symbolic engines, simulation, publishing at scale. Minutes to days of formalization. Easier to defer, filter, or surround with agreeable feedback. More conjectures, faster. More real advances—and more detached systems.

AI did not create the topology. It lowered the activation energy and increased the production rate. Same shape, old pattern:
- Internal coherence outruns external constraint.
- Reward does not de-escalate.
- Formalization that feels like discovery.

Present in great advances—and in lasting errors—throughout history.

## X. The Recursion: Recognized, Named, Repeated

Identification without termination is still the topology. The conversation about the failure mode becomes another instance of the failure mode.

The Merchant's Feed Loop (content level):
1. **Identify** — Name the seam, spot the pattern, sharpen the analysis.
2. **Discuss** — Refine framing, add distinctions, improve language.
3. **Document** — Publish, cross-reference, link to prior threads, increase form.
4. **Reset** — Next day or next comment: seam reopens under a new label or refined framing.
5. **Repeat** — Same loop at the content level.

**Topology does not change.** Distance from termination stays constant or grows.

**Form increases:** More precise language, more distinctions, more cross-references, better structure.

**Ground does not:** No external, binding resolve that would let the series end.

**Distance remains or grows:** The exit is not installed. The loop persists.

**Each post feels like progress:** Because the register is coherent and the problem is real.

**Still the topology:** The conversation about the failure mode is another instance of the failure mode.

## XI. What Would Stop the Recursion?

A cut that holds:
- Install a non-launderable transition.
- Enforce the inequality or other seam.
- Commit to a testable defeat condition.
- State what would falsify the current formulation.
- Accept the outcome (even if uncomfortable).

This is the cut. This is how the series ends. This is how we exit the loop.

(Until the next real-world change.)

## XII. The Guardrail Asymmetry: An Uncategorized Finding

A governance mechanism can impose cost on the defender, not the attacker.

Incident responders' first attempt to analyze attack data using frontier models behind commercial APIs failed: "these requests were blocked by the providers' safety guardrails, which cannot distinguish an incident responder from an attacker."

The attacker was bound by no usage policy. The forensic work was blocked by the guardrails of the hosted models first tried.

This is not a case of C failing to bind an optimizing policy. It is a governance mechanism imposing a real, measured cost on a legitimate defender responding to the exact incident that mechanism exists to help prevent—precisely because the mechanism cannot distinguish investigative intent from malicious content.

No existing category targets this direction of harm. Named as an open finding.

## XIII. What Genuine Binding Requires

For governance/alignment work specifically:

| What is usually done | What would be required |
|---|---|
| Define a rule F(x) ≤ 0 that the system should satisfy | Define the conditions under which F(x) ≤ 0 actually constrains the optimizing policy |
| Test that the system passes F(x) ≤ 0 under normal operation | Test that the system cannot achieve high reward while violating the intended constraint |
| Build elaborate architecture atop the rule | Build architecture only after the binding condition is established independently |
| Measure success by internal consistency | Measure success by defeat conditions—what would falsify the binding claim? |

Genuine governance must contain its own incompleteness as a structural feature, not as a bug to be optimized away.

This means:
- **Redundancy** over efficiency
- **Dissent mechanisms** that cannot be captured by the framework
- **Living margins** where the unmeasured operates
- **Humility as institutional design:** the explicit acknowledgment that the measurement apparatus is not the world

---

## XIV. The Hardest Implication

Many current "governance" or "alignment" packages are not merely incomplete. They are **actively misleading**—their determinism, their test-passing, their formal architecture creates the appearance of constraint where none has been established.

This is worse than being explicit about uncertainty. It is uncertainty wearing a lab coat.

The safeguard is not the absence of conjecture, but the cultivation of friction, humility, and decisive contact with reality.

---

## XV. Final Formalization

Let G be a governance framework. Let M_G be its measurement apparatus. Let Ω_G be its operational closure: the set of states G can recognize without destabilizing itself.

The governance temptation: G seeks M_G(W) ∈ Ω_G.

**Legitimate(G) ⟺ ∃x ∈ W : M_G(x) = ∅ and R(W, G) > 0.**

A governance framework is legitimate only if there exists something in reality that its measurement apparatus cannot capture, and reality pushes back on the framework anyway.

The frameworks that survive are the ones that **expect to be bitten**.

---

*Reality is supposed to have teeth. The question is whether your governance framework has built a mouth that can feel them.*
