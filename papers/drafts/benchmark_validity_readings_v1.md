# Benchmark-Validity Readings: Twenty-One Readings (Twenty Papers and One Genre) Read for Whether the Score Supports the Inference Drawn From It

*AI-assisted. Living research: staged, not peer reviewed, may be revised or withdrawn.*

**Scope of naming.** Twenty-one readings: twenty papers (eleven posted in October 2026, one in late September 2026, three added on 2026-11-09, one posted in June 2026 and two in November 2026, and three added on 2026-11-10, one first posted in May 2024 and revised through March 2026, one dated that day and one posted on 2026-11-05, and two posted on 2026-11-07 and 2026-11-10 and added on 2026-11-13), and a genre reading that reads six provenance sources (proof systems, an attestation specification and a report) together as one genre, added on 2026-11-11,  are each read in their own folder (the genre reading in `provenance_proofs_genre_reading/`), `benchmark_validity_readings_v1/<name>/README.md`. Authors are called "the authors" and are not named; organisations are named only as each paper names them. No intent is attributed to anyone. A thirteenth reading, of a working paper with no stable public copy, is not included (section 7).

**Disclosure.** AI-assisted (Claude, working under this project's own non-default standard: the operator's instructions, preregistration and primary-source checking discipline; not a vanilla configuration). Object selection, method, redaction and release decisions are the operator's. A single reader carried out every reading; no second reader. Where a paper evaluates models from the assisting model's developer or has authors affiliated with it, the individual reading says so, with that framework context. Readings are post hoc unless the folder holds a predictions file.

## 1. What this series is

Papers about whether a benchmark score supports the inference drawn from it: construct validity, dimensionality, differential functioning, saturation, scorer and judge independence, aggregation, contamination. It does not cover studies of how a harness changes scores (one entry, `continual_self_evolving_agents/`, reads such a paper only for whether its metrics support its claim), and it does not cover vendor capability posts.

## 3. Reading lens (applied during reading, not preregistered)

1. Is the construct the benchmark's own claim or the evaluator's? 2. Can the check fail (a null, a preregistered scope)? 3. What does the audited quantity differ from the reported one by? 4. Which alternative explanation does each test fail to separate? 5. Scorer and judge independence from the compared groups. 6. Does the generalization in the abstract match the sample in the limitations? 7. Is the lead larger than the noise implied by task counts, trials and clustering? 8. Does the ranking or headline survive a change in weights, or in which component carries it? 9. Can the headline metric be met by an extreme policy (escalate everything, refuse everything), and is the opposite error reported beside it? 10. Are the compared systems run at matched effort, harness and budget, and is any difference disclosed where the claim is made? 11. Is recall reported with precision, against a compute-matched baseline? 12. Does the abstract's comparison set include every comparator the evaluation table reports, and does the ratio it quotes stay when the others are added? (Questions 7-11 were added on 2026-11-09 and applied to the two readings added that day; question 12 on 2026-11-11 and applied to the nineteenth reading; the earlier readings were not re-read against them.)

## 4. Evidence tier and deeper-pass flag (convention from 2026-10-25)

Every reading ends with two lines. **Tier** states what it rests on: E1 executed (state what: arithmetic on the paper's reported numbers, a simulation, or the paper's own code or data), E2 read (state what was and was not read), E3 interpretation (name the flags that are interpretation). **Deeper pass** is `recommended`, `optional` or `no`, with the cheapest decisive test and whether it has been done. A deeper pass is recommended only when (a) a flag bears on the paper's headline claim, (b) the code or data needed is public, and (c) the test is small. A reading-only flag that settles the question (a metric defined against its own results) does not need one. Provenance caveat: the papers reached the reader mostly through reposts of abstracts, so counts of flags per reading describe that channel, not arXiv in general. The readings dated 2026-10-25 carry the lines retroactively.

## 5. Reading the flag counts

The number of flags in a reading is not a severity ranking. `raw_rag_agentic_memory/`, `nca_reasoning/`, `mcp_injector_fault_tolerance/` and `trust_statistical_gate/` are the contrast cases. The RAW-RAG reading carries as many flags as the readings of papers whose claims outran their tables, but they are mostly scope and reporting detail on a paper that states its own limits (an inherited baseline inconsistency, untested significance, reader dependence). The TRUST reading is of the same kind: nine flags on a paper that states its assumptions and limits, one of which is a result that stands (the safety result), with the others on scope, reporting and the distance between an asymptotic proof and the experiments' sample sizes. Read a reading by what its flags bear on (the headline claim, or the method's own reporting), not by how many there are.

### 5.1 Patterns that recur (added 2026-11-09; post hoc; twenty-one readings and one earlier public reading, not a rate)
Each pattern is listed only where a public reading in this series shows it. Patterns seen once so far are candidates, not findings.
1. **A lead inside the noise, with no intervals.** `pat_paper_assistant_tool/` (26 against 16 of 29 errors), `triage_path_dependence_chatbots/` (a headline exact test on pairs that repeat 28 cases across 13 chatbots), `fde_gym_delivery_benchmark/` (12 of 13 adjacent configuration pairs overlap), `theophyseval_physics_agents/` (a headline resting on a single run), `boxbox_harness_self_revision/` (intervals that resample runs over fixed tasks; a 10.6% lead with no task-level test), `posebench_docking_cofolding/` (run-to-run SD plotted, not test-set sampling; a PoseBusters gap of 3.7 points at n = 130), `tcsexam_theory_research_benchmark/` (a Lite-suite lead that is one task of five; ranks 3 to 6 within 2 index points), `trust_statistical_gate/` (a final-gain lead over the one working baseline with a difference under two standard errors in five of six settings; an allocator benefit at z 0.63).
2. **A headline carried by one component or one choice.** `ideascientist_ideation_agent/` (the margin comes from an in-domain novelty term), `fde_gym_delivery_benchmark/` (resolved counts dominated by the contact cost), `pairwise_difference_uq/` (a headline quantity defined so that it cannot be negative). Readings of composite indexes whose rank changes with the weights are not in the series yet.
3. **Comparisons run under different conditions.** `raw_rag_agentic_memory/` (the rank claim holds in matched settings and not on one benchmark), `fde_gym_delivery_benchmark/` (budget truncation read as capability), `pat_paper_assistant_tool/` (compute confounded with method), `triage_path_dependence_chatbots/` (sequence confounded with format), `boxbox_harness_self_revision/` (agent control confounded with context preservation, and the compared variant's review cadence close to the method's own).
4. **The evaluator belongs to the builders' loop, or shares a family with the compared systems.** `harmbench_psychometric_audit/` (judge-family confound not discussed), `pat_paper_assistant_tool/` (engine, baseline, grader and audit from one organisation), `triage_path_dependence_chatbots/` (simulator, checklist writer, rewriter and scorer from one developer family, three targets from it too), `boxbox_harness_self_revision/` (benchmark, method, baselines and task filter from one group, the filter model also the agent), `posebench_docking_cofolding/` (the authors define a new dataset variant and a new metric and rank with them), `alphaproof_code_signal_verification/` (the framework's authors run and report the evaluation; mitigated by open-sourcing and the maintainers' feedback), `tcsexam_theory_research_benchmark/` (the solver metric is referenced to an author organisation's model, which is also among the evaluated agents), the public reading `scientisttwo_release_reading_v1.md` (a headline that holds under the in-loop reviewer and not under the held-out one), and `synthcorp_benchmark_generator/` (the judge of the pipeline's semantic errors is also the top-ranked evaluated agent, with no judge validation reported and the gold-derivation panel unnamed).
5. **A headline number the paper's own tables or definitions do not reproduce.** `pairwise_difference_uq/` (a quantity that cannot be negative against reported negatives), `pat_paper_assistant_tool/` (a "34%" that is 34.5 points, and survey percentages that do not fit the stated cohort sizes), `boxbox_harness_self_revision/` (a per-dollar column 2.4 to 4.7 times above the ratio of the table's own means, reversing the lead), `posebench_docking_cofolding/` (stated test-set sizes of 85 and 122 against scored sizes of 84 and 100).
6. **A single-sided metric with a cheap extreme solution.** `triage_path_dependence_chatbots/`: emergency under-triage can be met by escalating everything, and the best endpoints on it over-triage most. Seen twice (the second: `trust_statistical_gate/`, where a zero false-acceptance rate is met by accepting nothing, which one baseline does and another nearly does, while the acceptance rate beside it, 0.19 to 0.45 times the greedy gate's, is not in the abstract).
7. **Recall without precision.** `pat_paper_assistant_tool/`: no false-positive rate against a compute-matched baseline. Seen once so far.
8. **A per-cost or per-unit metric built as a mean of per-item ratios.** `boxbox_harness_self_revision/`: cheap items dominate, a no-op earns the baseline value, and the ranking reverses on the ratio of means. Seen once so far.
9. **A running maximum over noisy measurements as the score.** `boxbox_harness_self_revision/`: best-so-far speedup with timing variation up to 10% and no re-measurement of the winning snapshot. Seen once so far.
10. **A stated sample size that is not the scored one.** `posebench_docking_cofolding/`: recounting from the released per-complex files gives 84 and 100 scored complexes for sets stated as 85 and 122. Seen once so far; needs released per-item data to detect.
11. **A metric rescaled against the compared set.** `posebench_docking_cofolding/`: an interaction score normalised with the best and worst compared methods, so values change with the roster. Seen once so far.
12. **The baseline set changes between versions of one paper.** `posebench_docking_cofolding/`: a competitive template-based method present in two versions is absent from the one with the stronger headline. Seen once so far.
13. **Conditions disclosed in the body but not carried into the abstract.** `alphaproof_code_signal_verification/` (a harness build, contracts for every cryptographic primitive, post-quantum healing not proved), `posebench_docking_cofolding/` (the abstract's "comparable ... baseline algorithms" without the input and condition asymmetries stated in the text) and `tcsexam_theory_research_benchmark/` (the abstract's support for Lite as a proxy, and open-conjecture progress, without the shared submissions, the selection on six agents and the retrieval stated in the body). Seen six times, in papers whose bodies are otherwise careful (the fifth: `trust_statistical_gate/`, where the planted-weakness starting harness sits in an appendix, and the abstract's lower bound on gain is asymptotic with the qualifier in the theorem discussion; the fourth: `provenance_proofs_genre_reading/`, where the semi-honest verifier, the quantisation level, the hardware mismatch and the report's limits sit in the body; the sixth: `synthcorp_benchmark_generator/`, where the abstract's near-zero error rate is scoped to deterministic checks counted per invocation while the body reports 6.2% and 12.7% semantic dependency violations, and the saturation motivation sits against a first release the conclusion calls simple for frontier models); this is a communication-layer pattern, not a defect in the method.
14. **Size of a produced artifact as the headline metric, and cost in labour without resource totals.** `alphaproof_code_signal_verification/`: 6.5 million proof lines; five people for four weeks; no compute, token or dollar totals and no baseline. Seen once so far.
15. **A post-hoc check whose reference may be amended toward the checked artifact.** `alphaproof_code_signal_verification/`: the specification can be amended when proofs fail, under guards; counts and direction of amendments were not in the text read. Seen once so far; needs the released log to test. A loose second instance: `synthcorp_benchmark_generator/` (a format-tolerant rescoring moves the top model's S4 accuracy from 88.8 to 94 and the second from 77.7 to 90, toward the reference, with the determination of the format-failure shares unstated).
16. **A compact suite validated against the data it was built from.** `tcsexam_theory_research_benchmark/`: most of Lite's tasks reuse the full suite's submissions and its list was chosen to preserve six agents' orderings, then rank agreement is offered as support; the out-of-sample part is 13 pairs and two agents. Seen once so far.
17. **A benchmark conditioned on a screening model's outcomes.** `tcsexam_theory_research_benchmark/`: more than 40 theorems retired after pilot runs solved them, stated by the authors as able to affect comparison with other agents. Seen once so far.
18. **Open-problem "resolutions" that are retrieval of published results.** `tcsexam_theory_research_benchmark/`: all accepted conjecture results adapt published counterexamples and proofs obtained on the network. Seen once so far.
19. **The abstract's comparison quotes the largest ratios in the paper's own comparison table.** `provenance_proofs_genre_reading/`: zkGPT's abstract reports 279x and 185x; its Table 3 also gives a 5.1x ratio against a third system and a time 1.38 times longer than a fourth, on a GPU, stated in the body as "30% slower" and attributed to hardware. The body is open about it. Seen once so far; recomputable from the printed table (question 12).
20. **A motivation word larger than the statement proved.** `provenance_proofs_genre_reading/`: three of six abstracts (legitimacy and authenticity; trust, accountability and compliance; "provably mitigate" with "minimal trust assumptions") where the body states a narrower claim or assumption. Seen in one reading, across several sources that are not independent of each other; not a rate.
21. **No control case for the process being certified.** `provenance_proofs_genre_reading/`: none of the proof systems read tests its proof against a substituted or wrong model and reports what it catches. Seen once so far, in a reading of partial texts.
22. **An asymptotic guarantee in the abstract whose explicit constant is vacuous at the sample size of the paper's own experiments.** `trust_statistical_gate/`: the abstract's lower bound on final gain comes from a proof with an explicit constant; at the main configuration it makes the multiplying factor 0.0025 at 40 draws per round and needs about 10,900 draws for a miss bound of one half (the pool has 200). Worst case, for arbitrarily small improvements; the empirical rates are not contradicted. Seen once so far; the same paper's error-rate guarantee does not have this problem.
23. **A candidate that can write the labels the gate scores.** `trust_statistical_gate/`: the paper's overfitting example has the proposer storing a validation question, its verified answer and the supporting facts in memory, and the candidate returns the stored answer when the question recurs; the paper's allowance for this is a hand-set correction strength, untested. Seen once so far; compare finding 5 of `papers/published/rsi_gate_audit_results_v1.md` (the provenance of the data the gate scores).
24. **Reference answers verified against the generator's own record, with agreement of same-family models offered as the external confirmation.** `synthcorp_benchmark_generator/`: gold programs are derived by an LLM debate against the simulation log and audited against that log, and the paper's outside evidence is that frontier agents match them on 90 to 94% of runs after a format-tolerant rescoring; the log is the pipeline's own record, and agreement between LLMs from overlapping training distributions is weak verification. Seen once so far; compare pattern 23 (the provenance of what a check scores) and finding 5 of `papers/published/rsi_gate_audit_results_v1.md`.

## 6. Verification, and what is not claimed

- **Quoted strings.** `quote_checks.py` tests every double-quoted string in each reading against the text layer of the paper's PDF (three extraction orders, normalised quotes and dashes). Twenty-one of twenty-one readings pass after correction (the six added on 2026-11-09 and 2026-11-10 and the one added on 2026-11-11 after one pass of correction each; the twentieth, added on 2026-11-13, passed on its final run, 18 of 18, and the twenty-first, added on 2026-11-13, 9 of 9; the nineteenth reads six sources, so its strings were tested against a combined PDF of five and, for the HTML specification, by exact text search); before correction, many phrases that had been put in quotation marks were close paraphrases, and those were either restored to the paper's wording or unquoted. The result of the final run is in each folder as `quote_checks_output.txt`. A pass is a lexical test: it does not show the reading's interpretation is right.
- **Source files.** Eleven of the twelve arXiv PDFs re-fetched (ten on 2026-10-26, `continual_self_evolving_agents` on 2026-10-29) are byte-identical to the copies read; the twelfth (`nca_reasoning`) differs in bytes but is word-for-word identical in text.
- **Scripts.** Every `checks*.py` re-ran in this layout and reproduced its committed output.
- **Not done.** No paper's code or data was run unless a reading says so; figures were mostly read as extracted text; the flags are leads for a deeper pass, not verdicts.

## 8. Limitations: what this does and does not establish

- Twenty-one readings (twenty papers and one multi-source genre reading) chosen by what reached the reader, not sampled; nothing here is a rate.
- One reader per paper (the assisting model, under this project's non-default standard); in some cases that model's developer is the subject (see each Disclosure).
- The series is a set of reading notes with scripts; it is not a literature review. An open item is a literature table for AI-judging-AI and benchmark-validity work, with inclusion criteria preregistered first. A second open item (added 2026-11-10): whether checks, verification, monitors and filters added after a system is built (post-hoc assurance) share the failure modes recorded here, or whether those modes belong to the genre of abstract and headline compression; a seed set is `alphaproof_code_signal_verification/`, `pat_paper_assistant_tool/`, `mcp_injector_fault_tolerance/` and `harmbench_psychometric_audit/`, and a control set of non-assurance readings would be needed to separate the two, with the coding rule preregistered first. On the single case read so far the modes sat in the abstract and headline, not in the machinery. A first genre pass over one kind of assurance layer, proofs and attestations of which process produced an output (`provenance_proofs_genre_reading/`, added 2026-11-11, partial reads, six sources, preregistered), found the same kind of distance, mostly at the level of comparator choice and motivation words, found the statements themselves mostly careful, and found no case where a proof is tested against a wrong or substituted process; the control set is still not built.
