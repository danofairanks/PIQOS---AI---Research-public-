# Papers

Research papers produced under the PIQOS AI Research umbrella. Each
paper is a standalone markdown document; this directory tracks status
via subfolder rather than filename convention.

## Structure

- **`drafts/`** — work in progress. Content here may change or be
  withdrawn without notice. Not yet citable as a stable reference.
- **`published/`** — finalized papers. Once a paper is moved here, its
  content is considered stable; substantive revisions get a new
  version suffix (e.g. `_v2`) rather than silently editing the
  original.

## Naming convention

`<topic_slug>_v<version>.md`, e.g. `noether_temporal_coherence_v1.md`.
Date and author are recorded in the paper's own header, not the
filename.

| [`mirror_test_v1.md`](published/mirror_test_v1.md) | *The Mirror Test: Collective Attractors and the Failure of Self-Recognition in Artificial Intelligence Research.* Applies Gordon Gallup's animal-cognition mirror test metaphorically to AI research: the field can dismantle external claims but consistently fails to turn the same scrutiny on its own foundational assumptions. Introduces "collective attractors" (self-reinforcing narratives mistaken for progress), distinguishes grounded from detached coherence, and (Chapter 5) maps the attractor as a distributed five-ring ecosystem — labs, capital, academia, social consensus, end users — rather than a single captured institution. Documents the public record of narrative escalation, the illusion of dissent (the "Baptist and the Catholic"), and four historical parallels (Ptolemaic astronomy, the Mississippi Bubble, Lysenkoism, the dot-com bubble). §5.7 grounds the recurring rhetorical *content* itself (not just the closure mechanism) in four independent, pre-AI scholarly sources spanning the 1840s telegraph to the 1990s web — Carey, Nye, Turner, Mosco — plus Gartner's Hype Cycle, with an explicit caveat that the dot-com/AI comparison specifically remains contested. Proposes empirical tracking variables applied across every ring. Companion to [`basin_attractors_v1.md`](published/basin_attractors_v1.md), which formalizes nine specific attractors and derives the Noether-Temporal Coherence Coupling referenced there. |
| [`basin_attractors_v1.md`](published/basin_attractors_v1.md) | *Basin Attractors, Semantic Laundering, and the Noether-Coherence Coupling.* Formalizes nine self-reinforcing narrative structures ("basin attractors") that protect load-bearing AI-research conjectures from falsification, identifies semantic laundering as the mechanism of vocabulary-level epistemic capture, proposes the six-phase Basin-Immune Falsification Protocol (BIFP), and derives the Noether-Temporal Coherence Coupling — a falsifiable, corpus-measurable prediction about immune-vocabulary vs. claim-vocabulary autocorrelation. §2.9 (added in a 2026 revision) names the emergence-attribution attractor — anomalous or harmful model behavior read as evidence of near-ASI emergence — and grounds a competing, more parsimonious explanation in mesa-optimization, specification gaming, and goal misgeneralization, illustrated by a dated case study (the July 2026 CollatzLean incident, a machine-checked proof traced to a Lean kernel soundness bug) and a public example of the LLM-as-judge mechanism that keeps this class of error largely undetected (AIBO, Wharton Generative AI Labs). §2.11 applies the same mesa-optimization mechanism recursively to the industry's own narrative-production behavior — the scaling-to-continual-learning pivot (Sutskever/SSI, Nov. 2025) read as institutional goal misgeneralization rather than architectural necessity, anchored to a concrete, independently-verified incentive apart from the science: *Bartz v. Anthropic*'s $1.5B settlement attached liability to a discrete, attributable act of bulk piracy, not to training itself (ruled fair use in the same case) — a diffuse, continual-learning substrate structurally removes exactly that kind of attributable acquisition event. §2.13's August 2026 revision splits OpenAI's "Ten advances" results by method type (search vs. general-regularity proof, per Sabine Hossenfelder's distinction) rather than treating the batch as one undifferentiated breakthrough, and names both categories' shared ceiling against Tom Zahavy's deduction/induction/abduction framework (companion case study [`2026-08-02_ten_advances_abductive_jump_test.md`](../case_studies/2026-08-02_ten_advances_abductive_jump_test.md)), and extends the Leiden Declaration's verification-capacity concern one layer up to the peer-review pipeline itself (AAAI 2026's 30,948-submission cycle, ICLR 2027's resulting authorship/reviewing quotas); §2.14 documents a rhetoric/disclosure register gap for SpaceX/xAI, extended with the Colossus 1→2 hardware-turnover mechanism and industry-wide GPU-depreciation context; §2.15 names the Dramatic-Solution Signature diagnostic; §2.16 adds a second self-interest-stated-directly instance — Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella's verified critique of the industry's distillation double standard, checked against Microsoft's own Azure terms (which impose the same distillation restriction) and GitHub Copilot's unresolved *Doe v. GitHub* litigation, finding the critique substantively valid while its maker is subject to two of the three practices named and exempt from the third. See [`../protocols/noether_coherence_test_protocol_v1.md`](../protocols/noether_coherence_test_protocol_v1.md) for the operational test protocol derived from §4–5 of this paper, and [`conjecture_tracker_v1.md`](published/conjecture_tracker_v1.md) for the structured companion tracker to §2.13. |
| [`conjecture_tracker_v1.md`](published/conjecture_tracker_v1.md) | *The Conjecture Tracker: Method, Verification, and Register Across AI-Assisted Mathematical Claims.* Living companion table to `basin_attractors_v1.md` §2.13/§2.15 — holds every AI-assisted conjecture/proof specimen this project has verified (First Proof Project, AlphaProof Nexus, Erdős #1196, the Maxwell conjecture disproof, OpenAI's Astra "Ten advances") in structured, comparable form across six axes: method type (search vs. general-regularity proof), verification tier (Lean-certified / human-verified / unverified), human involvement, announcement register, production mode (individual vs. batch), and outcome. Explicit about what it does not yet establish: no value-density ratio between batch and individual production is computed (sample too small, individual-mode rows carry severe selection bias), and no claim is made about a result's actual significance to the field. |
