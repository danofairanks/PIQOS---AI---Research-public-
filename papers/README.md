# Papers

Research papers produced under the PIQOS AI Research umbrella. Each
paper is a standalone markdown document; this directory tracks status
via subfolder rather than filename convention.

## Structure

- **`drafts/`** — work in progress. Content here may change or be
  withdrawn without notice. Not yet citable as a stable reference.
- **`published/`** — finalized papers. Once a paper is moved here, its
  content is considered stable; substantive revisions get a new
  version suffix (e.g. `_v2`) rather than silently editing the
  original.

## Naming convention

`<topic_slug>_v<version>.md`, e.g. `noether_temporal_coherence_v1.md`.
Date and author are recorded in the paper's own header, not the
filename.

Introduces "collective attractors" (self-reinforcing narratives mistaken for progress), distinguishes grounded from detached coherence, and (Chapter 5) maps the attractor as a distributed five-ring ecosystem — labs, capital, academia, social consensus, end users — rather than a single captured institution. Documents the public record of narrative escalation, the illusion of dissent (the "Baptist and the Catholic"), and four historical parallels (Ptolemaic astronomy, the Mississippi Bubble, Lysenkoism, the dot-com bubble). §5.7 grounds the recurring rhetorical *content* itself (not just the closure mechanism) in four independent, pre-AI scholarly sources spanning the 1840s telegraph to the 1990s web — Carey, Nye, Turner, Mosco — plus Gartner's Hype Cycle, with an explicit caveat that the dot-com/AI comparison specifically remains contested. Proposes empirical tracking variables applied across every ring. Companion to [`basin_attractors_v1.md`](published/basin_attractors_v1.md), which formalizes nine specific attractors and derives the Noether-Temporal Coherence Coupling referenced there. |
