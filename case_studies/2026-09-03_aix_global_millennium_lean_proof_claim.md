# Real-Time Specimen Analysis: AIX Global / Seed IQ — "Solving the Six Remaining Clay Millennium Problems" (2026)

### A kernel-checked Lean 4 package whose headline theorems never derive the six actual Clay-official statements — three of them the identical, content-free arithmetic induction — independently reconstructed by outside reviewers after direct technical challenge went unanswered

---

## Executive Summary

AIX Global, Inc. (Denise Holt & Denis Ovseyenko), the same organization behind the self-reported "100%, BEATING ARC-AGI-3" claim this project already documented in [`2026-08-04_aix_seed_iq_arc_agi_3_claim.md`](2026-08-04_aix_seed_iq_arc_agi_3_claim.md), published a 54-page paper and a bundled, downloadable Lean 4 proof package claiming to have solved all six remaining Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize problems, requesting the $6M prize (the Clay Institute's own published $1M-per-problem total). The claim is unusually checkable: AIX supplied an actual, runnable, kernel-verified formal-proof artifact rather than only prose. Read directly, source-line by source-line, the package does not do what the paper claims. None of the six actual Clay-official theorem statements is derived anywhere in the file. Three of the six headline theorems — for Hodge, Navier–Stokes, and Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer, spanning algebraic geometry, PDE analysis, and arithmetic geometry with no known relationship to one another — are the identical, content-free proof of `0 < n + 1`. Two others (Riemann, P vs NP) are real, correctly-typed *conditional* theorems whose decisive hypotheses are never supplied. Yang–Mills has no formal statement of the actual Clay problem anywhere in the file at all. The bundled self-audit script checks the wrong theorem for three of the six problems, reporting closure on definitions that were never proven. Every individual lemma the package actually proves is genuinely, correctly proven — "0 sorry, 0 project axiom" is literally true — which is exactly what makes this a clean instance of a mechanism this project's framework already names: performed rigor (the formatting and kernel-checked mechanics of a rigorous proof) standing in for demonstrated rigor (the argument actually closing on the claimed target). Directly challenged with the specific missing identifiers, the claimant repeated a "proprietary quantum computation supplies the certificate; the verifier remains sovereign" defense without ever supplying or naming a theorem that connects that certificate to the target statements. Two independent, named, non-anonymous outside reviewers — reaching the artifact from an unrelated LinkedIn post, with no visible connection to this project — arrived at the identical finding on their own, one of them coining a precise name for the mechanism ("local correctness without global target fidelity") independently of this project's own vocabulary for it. The confronting exchange ends, per operator testimony, with both authors having become unreachable to the confronting party — a real, if unresolved, close.

## The Specimen: What Was Claimed, and What Was Actually Supplied

**The claim.** "Demonstrating Quantum Value by Solving the Six Remaining Clay Millennium Problems," Denise Holt & Denis Ovseyenko, AIX Global, Inc., DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22179463, dated August 30, 2026 — a 54-page paper closing with a request that the Clay Mathematics Institute recognize the result and award the associated prizes. It was shared on LinkedIn by Denis O. ("Fintech Professional | AI/ML Solution Architect," AIX Global's Co-Founder and Chief Innovation Officer — the same individual behind the ARC-AGI-3 claim this project already tracked), alongside a terminal screenshot showing a `lake build` / `lake env lean` run, and a downloadable zip (`MillenniumLean_ClayProof_20260901.zip`) presented as the complete, kernel-checked Lean 4 proof companion.

**What makes this specimen different from most this project tracks.** The claim is not only prose or a leaderboard screenshot — it is an actual, runnable formal-verification artifact. This case study is built on a direct, line-by-line reading of the supplied `Millennium.lean` (404 lines), `Tower.lean` (30 lines), `print_axioms.lean`, the bundled `audit.py`, `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md`, and `WHY_THIS_IS_PROOF.md` — not a summary of the paper's own description of them. The zip's contents were independently re-downloaded and byte-compared (`diff -rq`) against the original extraction in a later verification pass; both matched.

## The Central Finding: None of the Six Clay-Official Statements Is Derived

Problem by problem, checked directly against the file:

1. **Riemann Hypothesis.** A real, correctly-typed *conditional* theorem: given a self-adjoint operator, a correspondence function into its spectrum, and the required hypotheses, the Riemann Hypothesis follows — a valid Lean formalization of the long-known Hilbert–Pólya heuristic. The operator, the correspondence function, and the hypotheses are never constructed or supplied anywhere in the file. The actual Riemann Hypothesis target is never closed.
2. **Yang–Mills.** The one line calling itself the Yang–Mills theorem is a tautology (`m` equals some already-positive `c`, therefore `m` is positive) requiring no field theory whatsoever. Unlike the other five problems, no formal statement of the real Clay problem (rigorously construct 4D SU(3) Yang–Mills theory and prove a mass gap) appears anywhere in the file to be checked against.
3. **Navier–Stokes.** The file does contain a faithful, carefully-built formalization of the actual Clay statement — and it is never proven. The theorem the package names `navier_stokes` proves nothing about it; it proves a generic, unrelated arithmetic induction (below).
4. **Hodge Conjecture.** No formal statement of the actual Hodge conjecture (a claim about every smooth complex projective variety) exists in the file at all. What exists is a single verified arithmetic fact about one fixed pair of spaces — real and correct, but not the conjecture. The theorem named `hodge` proves the same unrelated generic induction as Navier–Stokes.
5. **Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer.** Same pattern as Navier–Stokes: a real, careful formalization of the actual Clay statement exists and is never proven; the theorem named `bsd` proves the same generic induction.
6. **P vs NP.** Another real, correctly-typed conditional theorem: given an unconditional proof-complexity lower bound and a "bridge" hypothesis connecting it to computational complexity, P ≠ NP follows. The bridge hypothesis — exactly the deep, unsolved content real complexity theory has never established — is never proven or supplied; it is an assumed premise, not a theorem.

**The single most direct piece of evidence in the file:** three of the six headline theorems — for Hodge, Navier–Stokes, and BSD — are the identical proof of the identical statement, `∀ n, Tower.declInv n`, where `Tower.declInv n` is defined as `0 < n + 1`. Three of the most famous open problems in mathematics, spanning fields with no known relationship to each other, *proved* by the same one-line arithmetic fact is not a matter of interpretation — it is directly readable from the source. The file contains a comment describing a *corrected* proof machinery meant to replace this trivial induction; that machinery is defined and never actually used by any of the three theorems it was supposedly built to fix.

**The bundled self-audit script checks the wrong theorem for at least three of the six problems.** `audit.py`'s own dictionary of what to verify points, for Hodge, Navier–Stokes, and BSD, at the disconnected numeric fact or the bare unproven statement — never at the theorems the paper actually claims prove those results. Running the script and seeing a theorem reported "closed" would reasonably, but incorrectly, read as confirming the conjecture itself is closed. Whether this is an oversight or a design choice cannot be determined from the artifact alone, and this case study does not choose between them — it is reported as a precise, independently checkable fact either way.

## What Is Actually True — and Why That Makes the Inference the Interesting Part

Every individual lemma this reading checked is genuinely, correctly proven, using real machinery from Lean's `mathlib` library. The formalizations of the real target statements — for Riemann, Navier–Stokes, and BSD specifically — are careful and faithful to the actual Clay problems, more so than most self-published specimens this project has tracked. "0 sorry, 0 project axiom" is literally true, exactly as the package's own `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md` states.

What does not follow is the paper's own closing inference: *"By the definition of proof in a formal system, all six Clay Millennium problems are proved here."* A conditional theorem with unsupplied hypotheses, or a generic template theorem never connected to its target, carries the identical "0 sorry, 0 axiom" footprint as an unconditional proof of that target would — while proving something categorically weaker. The Lean kernel verifies that a proof term inhabits its *stated* type. It says nothing about whether that stated type is the theorem the paper's title, abstract, and prize request claim it is.

This project's own framework already names this mechanism precisely. `basin_attractors_v2.md` §2.5 documents formal-looking output substituting for a genuinely closed argument at scale in the broader AI-research literature; `laundered_vocabulary_v1.md`'s **Performed Rigor vs. Demonstrated Rigor** distinction states it exactly: *"Performed rigor is the presence of the formatting of rigorous work... Demonstrated rigor is whether the argument actually survives an independent party attempting to re-derive it from the stated premises."* A kernel-checked Lean file with zero `sorry`s is about as strong a performance of rigor as exists in mathematics — and, as this specimen shows directly, that performance and the actual closure of the claimed target can come apart completely, even inside a fully mechanically verified artifact.

## The Defense, and an Unanswered Soundness Challenge

Directly challenged (screenshots inspected: an operator exchange, and separately, a LinkedIn thread under a third party's post) with the specific missing identifiers — the six theorem names the paper's own table claims closes the six problems, and the `gftqc_`-prefixed certificate identifiers the paper describes as carrying the decisive computed content into the kernel — Denis O.'s reply repeats, across multiple threads and multiple confronting parties, the same framing: *"the verifier remains sovereign. The historical obstruction was the inability to compute the decisive objects needed to close these arguments. The quantum capability is precisely what changes that."* AIX Global's own prior claim in this space — an April 2026 "fault-tolerant quantum computing breakthrough" — was already found by the specialist outlet postquantum.com not to survive contact with its own paper's methodology, per this project's already-published prior case study on this organization.

The defense is a real architectural possibility in principle: a proprietary computation supplying a result, with a public, machine-checkable bridge connecting that result to the formal target, is a legitimate design. The problem is narrower and directly checkable: **that bridge does not appear in the supplied artifact.** None of the six named "closed final" theorems the paper's own table claims (`riemann_final` through `p_neq_np_final`), and none of the `gftqc_`-prefixed certificate identifiers the paper says those theorems consume, exists anywhere in the file the DOI serves — confirmed by direct text search, repeated after a later "v2" package release with the same negative result. When asked directly and precisely — name the theorem, not the unproven target statement, not the generic arithmetic induction, that actually connects a governed-computation certificate to the real content of Navier–Stokes or BSD — no such theorem was ever named. `gftqc_` is worth flagging on its own terms: "governed fault-tolerant quantum computing" is genuine-sounding technical vocabulary attached to a component that, checked directly, is not present in the artifact under discussion — a clean instance of `basin_attractors_v2.md` §2.8's Semantic Laundering, borrowed precision applied to a claim the borrowed term does not actually make checkable.

## Independent Convergence, Reached Without This Project's Involvement

The sharpest evidence in this specimen did not come from this project's own reading. A separate LinkedIn post by Miklós Molnár (unconnected to AIX Global, running his own ongoing, dated analytical series that predates this specimen by three weeks) drew Denis O. into the same defense, in a third venue, with the same stock Zenodo link. Two named, non-anonymous outside reviewers then reconstructed this specimen's central finding independently, from scratch, with no visible access to any prior audit of the artifact:

Molnár wrote, unprompted, in a LinkedIn reply: *"My current reading is that it does not yet look like a complete end-to-end formal proof of the six Millennium claims: in several places the decisive mathematical content appears to remain as an explicit premise, a target definition, or otherwise outside the closed Lean term."* In a companion long-form article, he coins and writes a precise name for the mechanism, independently of this project's own vocabulary for it: **"local correctness without global target fidelity."** *"Many individual Lean objects may be perfectly valid. The kernel can accept them, the project can contain no sorry, and there may be no custom axioms at all — yet none of those facts establishes that the complete chain ends in the theorem advertised outside the formal system."* This restates, in Molnár's own words and reasoning, exactly the "0 sorry, 0 axiom... What is false is the inference drawn from it" finding above.

Alexey Yashin (Tech Lead, NEP Group), independently, names the exact missing-identifier class this case study's own reading already found absent: *"Current, V2 package — at least for RH — is missing a module containing `gftqc_operator`, its self-adjointness proof, `gftqc_corr`, and `riemann_final`."* Pressed by Yashin on how the computation is actually validated in Lean, Molnár's reply is worth quoting directly for its method: *"The Zenodo Lean package was independently verified by SHA-256 hash to be the same artifact, then searched for the three declarations you cited... None appears in the published ZIP... the expectation is simple: the proprietary computation does not need to be reproduced in Lean, but the truth-bearing bridge from the computed certificate to the final mathematical claim must be publicly machine-checkable. In the currently published artifact, that bridge does not appear verifiable... If a newer artifact contains those declarations and a closed `p_neq_np_final`, the assessment should be updated immediately."*

Three independent verification passes — this project's own direct reading, a second independent re-verification pass after a fresh session, and Molnár and Yashin's fully external review — converge on the same identifier-level facts about the same artifact.

## What This Case Study Does Not Claim

Does not establish bad faith — a "corrected" proof machinery sitting unused next to the trivial induction it was meant to replace is consistent with genuine confusion or an incomplete refactor, not only with deliberate misdirection, and this case study does not choose between them. Does not establish that AIX Global's private, undisclosed computation layer produced nothing real — that layer is not in the supplied artifact and was not checked here; the finding is specifically that nothing from that layer reaches the Lean file as an actual proof of any of the six targets, whatever it privately computed. Does not verify the underlying quantum-computing methodology behind AIX's broader claims, which is out of scope for a proof-artifact reading. Does not establish that Denis O. personally authored the Lean package as distinct from amplifying AIX Global's own published work. Does not establish that Miklós Molnár or Alexey Yashin have any connection to this project or to each other beyond what their own public exchange shows. Does not establish which of "blocked" or "account/post deletion" occurred, or that either author's reason for going silent was the technical challenge specifically — both are stated as what they are: a real, observed outcome (unreachability) and the operator's own reasoning for one interpretation of it, not an independently confirmed mechanism.
